# The Pearl Beneath the Tide

*A novella of The Anchor's Secret through Session 07*

## Chapter One — The Wall at Tidewatch

[[World/Locations/Verdant Strand/New Penmaris/New Penmaris|New Penmaris]] was a city built on top of a wound.

Old Penmaris lay beneath it: drowned streets, collapsed warehouses, sea walls split by the [[World/Events/Flaming Retribution|Flaming Retribution]]. Refugees crowded into half-finished districts while cranes raised new roofs over old graves. Every day the city became larger. Every night the tide reminded it what had been lost.

The Tidewatch Ward remained behind a wall.

It had taken the worst of the magical storm. Monsters came in from the sea. Fog moved where no wind carried it. The Iron Tide Regiment sealed the district, placed wards over its gates, and spent a year losing soldiers in every attempt to reclaim it.

Eventually, the city ran out of soldiers it could afford to lose.

So it sent adventurers.

Arthur entered Tidewatch because the council had asked him to hunt a demon. He was a lion beastkin with a silver shield, a young paladin carrying the last words of the priest who raised him: *Be the light when all else burns away.* Beside him came Skirk, a fire-forged kobold who had once lifted a dock anchor in grief and smashed monsters until none remained. The two had defended Penmaris together during the first invasion. They had been chosen because someone had seen what they did when stronger warriors ran.

Mistral entered with pieces of a pirate treasure map and a past he had spent years trying to outwalk. He had once been Vaerion Lumestris, a smuggler who told himself that moving relics and people through the dark was mercy. A man died because of that lie. The monks gave him discipline, and he gave himself a new name: the wind that cuts and then clears.

Lil John wanted Coralite, the strange magical coral appearing in the city's markets. If the monsters of Tidewatch produced it, then perhaps it could be shaped into shields and weapons for the people rebuilding outside the wall.

Jezrek Sprocketmonger carried a broken star-chart compass salvaged from Old Penmaris. When opened, it cast a map of impossible lights into the air. He came because untamed magic was an invitation he had never learned to refuse.

Xander Floodbringer came for a fisherman whose name he had never needed to ask. They had shared silent mornings, cast lines into the same gray water, and understood one another through the absence of questions. The man's abandoned tackle had been found near Tidewatch.

Xander had died once already, long before the Retribution. He had known centuries of peace beside his lost wife, children, and comrades in Miraine's realm. Then the goddess sent him back to a mortal world. He had never forgiven her. He still used her power to help people.

Frederick Wainshore opened the gate.

The old soldier gave them potions, tracking runes, a sending stone, and one piece of advice.

“If you see the fog move against the wind, run.”

Then the wall closed behind them.

## Chapter Two — The Salty Dog

Tidewatch did not feel abandoned.

It felt occupied by things that had learned to be quiet.

Bone crabs scuttled through the street, humanoid skulls fused into their shells. A frog swollen with tentacles lunged from the mist. The six fought their way to an old tavern called the [[World/Locations/Verdant Strand/New Penmaris/Salty Dog|Salty Dog]], where Skirk remembered noise, cheap drink, and dockworkers who had believed the future would resemble the past.

The tavern was battered but defensible. More importantly, someone had written on its walls.

Mistral read the thieves' cant beneath the grime. The marks belonged to the [[World/Factions/The Verdant Strand/Tidecutters|Tidecutters]], a pirate crew led by Captain Garrick Tidebreaker. Again and again, the graffiti mentioned a pearl.

One message took the shape of a riddle.

*Where rum runs dry and secrets sleep,*

*Behind the Dog, the gold runs deep.*

In the alley, the party found a broken clockwork mechanism built around four elemental symbols. Sea. Flame. Stone. Wind. Jezrek repaired what time and salt had damaged. Mistral supplied the criminal patience to see where the mechanism lied. The wall opened.

The vault beneath the tavern had not been left unguarded. Armor walked without bodies. A rug tried to smother the living. A treasure chest opened a mouth.

The party destroyed them and divided what the Tidecutters had hidden. Arthur found armor and a warhammer worthy of the vows he was still learning to carry. Skirk gained a chain and anchor that answered storm with lightning. Mistral wrapped thunder around his fists. Jezrek took up a crossbow built for unreasonable ideas. Lil John and Xander found tools suited to hunters and servants of the divine.

They also found another piece of Mistral's map.

It pointed south.

That night they watched the ward from the Salty Dog's roof. Coral-mutated figures moved through the fog. Shapes crossed broken streets and vanished behind seawalls. At dawn, a thunderous pulse flashed to the south.

They followed it to a Stormcaller ritual.

Cultists gathered around an orb containing a living storm. Every moment it remained whole, more creatures answered its call. Skirk drove his anchor into the ritual while the others held the cultists back. The orb shattered. Lightning crawled over the wet iron of Skirk's weapon and stayed there.

When silence returned, a hooded figure stepped from the ruins and accused them of being pirates.

Before they could settle the misunderstanding, actual pirates arrived.

The battle happened inside magical silence. Men died without hearing their own weapons. Rocks crossed the air soundlessly. When it ended, the hooded stranger gave his name: Veyric Thorne.

He led them to what remained of the Iron Tide Regiment inside the ward.

## Chapter Three — The Coralmarked

The survivors called themselves the [[World/Factions/The Verdant Strand/Iron Tide (Coralmarked)|Coralmarked]].

They had entered Tidewatch in earlier expeditions and never escaped. Coral grew from their skin in ridges and fans. Some bore it along their shoulders, others across their faces. Every day within the ward increased the chance that another growth would appear.

It was not a disease.

It was a curse.

Xander found his fisherman among them.

The man lived. That should have made the discovery simple. Instead, coral had rooted into his flesh, and no one knew whether taking him beyond the wall would save him, kill him, or carry Tidewatch's corruption into the city.

Veyric explained the ward's factions.

In the northern docks, labor automatons had awakened into murderous independence. In the south, the Stormcaller cult had begun mutating in ways more infernal than coral. Pirates entered from the coast without suffering any transformation at all. At the center waited the [[World/Factions/The Verdant Strand/Pearlbound|Pearlbound]].

Every thinking creature that died in Tidewatch rose again after twelve hours unless its body was burned. Pearls sealed the corpse's eyes. Light moved beneath its veins. Coral armored it. Whatever mind had once lived inside did not return.

Dozens of Pearlbound surrounded the Temple of Miraine at the ward's center.

The cultists had been trying to draw them away. The pirates were moving toward the same place. A great light had flashed from the temple just before the storm and monsters swallowed Tidewatch.

Something waited beneath it.

The party chose to parley with the Tidecutters. Partly because pirates were hunting Mistral, partly because his map belonged to their secret, and partly because fighting every enemy in Tidewatch at once was a reliable way to join the Pearlbound.

The journey to the coast took days through streets that folded around floodwater and fog. Every night the curse tested them. Every morning Skirk scratched at skin that might or might not be changing.

The Tidecutters had their own version of the ward's fall.

Six months earlier, pirates had gone to steal the Pearl beneath Miraine's temple. The catastrophe followed. Whether their theft caused it, interrupted it, or merely happened at the same moment, no one knew. But the Pearl was no jewel. It was a vessel of weather, a power that had watched over Penmaris. Xander suspected it might be part of the reason the island had survived the Flaming Retribution at all.

The pirates wanted it back.

The party made a bargain they did not trust and staged signs that their camp had been overrun. Then both groups began moving toward the temple, each pretending the race had not already begun.

## Chapter Four — Racing the Tide

The fog tried to turn them aside.

Shapes moved within it. The mist thickened into monsters when watched too closely. Jezrek's star compass burned false constellations into the gray and kept them moving south.

They found a battlefield before they found the temple.

Iron Tide soldiers, Stormcaller cultists, Pearlbound corpses, and a demon fought across a broken avenue. A covered wagon moved behind the cult under careful guard. The party could have passed unseen and gained ground on the pirates.

They did not.

Arthur and Skirk had been sent into Tidewatch to find a demon. Xander could not walk past soldiers in need. The others had spent too long becoming a company to pretend that speed mattered more than people.

They entered the battle.

The demon fell. The Stormcaller leader was defeated. Three Iron Tide soldiers survived because the party stopped. An unconscious priestess lay beneath an enchantment Jezrek could identify but not break. Skirk's suggested cure—a solid strike—proved equally ineffective.

Pirates were closing.

The party entrusted the priestess to the soldiers and ran.

A river cut across the road where a bridge had collapsed. Support pillars stood in fifty feet of rushing water. Jezrek studied the wreckage. Skirk began breaking lumber to the sizes he called for. Together they built a new crossing in minutes, better than the one the ward had lost.

Then Jezrek folded the bridge into his pack so the pirates could not use it.

The fog delayed them again, but the compass pulled them through. At last the [[World/Locations/Verdant Strand/New Penmaris/Temple of Miraine|Temple of Miraine]] rose before them beneath a complete dome of orange-glowing [[World/Items/Coralite|Coralite]].

More than fifty Pearlbound circled it.

There was no path through them.

Xander walked forward.

The dead turned their pearl-covered eyes toward him. Then they parted.

No command was spoken. No spell shone around him. The Pearlbound simply recognized something in the returned servant of Miraine and opened a road.

The others crowded close. Skirk walked nearly beneath Xander's legs.

At the Coralite shell, Lil John's search found its answer on a scale too large to harvest. Skirk hooked his anchor into the lattice and tore open a hole. Xander discovered that the dome was a sphere whose center extended below ground, and that a natural seam could be widened without destroying it. Once the party passed through, he repaired the damage behind them.

The temple waited inside a shallow pool of seawater.

It was quiet. Pearl walls held the cold. The altar was shaped like the keel of a boat. Miraine's shield-and-wave symbol watched from the wall behind it.

Mistral's map gave instructions.

He placed his palm against the shield, spoke the goddess's name, and pressed.

Water spiraled out of the altar. Stone divided. Cold air rolled up a staircase descending into the Gloamways.

The pirates were somewhere behind them.

The party went down.

## Chapter Five — The Knight in the Water

The hidden chamber was flooded to the waist.

Pale blue light moved beneath the surface. Four levers lined the walls, each marked with an element: flame, sun, thunder, and another power hidden at the far end. A fountain stood upon a raised dais. Its pedestal was empty.

At the base of the dais rested [[World/Items/Artifacts/The Pearl|the Pearl]].

It was nearly the size of a man's head.

The water moved.

The Knight of Miraine appeared everywhere at once. A sword crossed Arthur's companions before any of them could set their feet. Radiance cut through armor. Water gathered into the shape of a warrior, shattered under blows, and flowed back together.

Jezrek created a rowboat beneath himself and began firing from its absurd safety. Xander rose on spectral wings. Skirk raged and forced his way through the water. Mistral ran for the Pearl.

Every wound they dealt closed. The chamber itself restored the Knight, thirty heartbeats of life returned at a time. Brute force could not win.

Then fire struck near the flame lever, and the symbol glowed.

Xander called radiant power, and the sun answered.

Skirk's wet anchor cracked with lightning, and the thunder mark pulsed.

The chamber was not merely a battlefield. It was a lock.

The Knight cut Mistral down before he reached the dais. He fell beneath the water, blood rising around him in thin red threads.

Jezrek piloted his boat to the first lever. A bolt of fire unlocked it. He pulled. Water began draining from the chamber.

The Knight regenerated.

Xander reached the far wall and opened the sun path. The water dropped to their ankles. Skirk smashed the lightning lever with his anchor. The chamber emptied in a rush, and the Knight's ability to restore itself collapsed with it.

Now every blow remained.

Skirk struck again and again, lightning breaking the watery shape apart. Jezrek's crossbow burned holes through its guard. Xander attacked from above while watching Mistral's still body. The last lever opened. The final channels drained.

The Knight tried to reform.

Skirk's anchor came down in a storm-bright arc.

The guardian shattered.

Its sword struck the dry stone with a heavy thud. What had appeared to be a divine longsword resolved into a pirate cutlass reshaped by water magic.

The Knight had hesitated when it attacked Xander. The Pearlbound had parted for him. Somewhere in the mystery of his unwanted return, Miraine had placed him closer to this secret than he wished to be.

Mistral lay unconscious near the dais.

The Pearl waited beside him.

Behind them, through the tunnels of the Gloamways, came the sound of pirates drawing near.

The six reclaimers had entered Tidewatch to hunt a demon, find a friend, follow a map, gather Coralite, and chase impossible magic. They had found all those things braided around a single object beneath the drowned city.

Now they had only moments to decide whether the Pearl was a weapon, a ward, a heart—or an anchor holding New Penmaris above the sea.
