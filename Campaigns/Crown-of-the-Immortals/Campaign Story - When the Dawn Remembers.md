# When the Dawn Remembers

*A novella of Crown of the Immortals through Session 27*

## Prologue — The Names Beneath the Names

In the Second Age, when the dawn still answered mortals directly, the [[World/Religion/Deific-Beings/La Pelu (Sun Swords)|La Pelu]] walked beneath the sun.

They were not demons then. They were not the bedtime monsters later ages made of them. They were the Sun Swords of Raythelion: judges, healers, warriors, and stewards entrusted with power vast enough to make mercy a law of nature.

Then [[World/Characters/Vorath the Betrayer|Vorath]] listened to the dark.

Erabose offered the old champion a world without restraint. Syrith the Shadowed and Maldran the Deceiver followed him. Light broke against light. The conflict became the [[World/Events/The War of Fractured Dawn|War of Fractured Dawn]], and cities burned beneath powers that had once been created to protect them.

Four uncorrupted La Pelu survived the worst of it. A bard and diplomat carried the Beacon's Blade. A warrior-king bore the Heart of the Flame. A general wore the Infernal Breeze Gauntlets. An oracle guarded the Mirror of Ashes. Together they formed the [[World/Factions/The Veilcant Guard|Veilcant Guard]] and helped cast Vorath down.

Victory did not make the world safe.

The Veilcant Guard broke the Llano—the Song of Creation—into hidden verses. They sealed away their relics. They founded orders that would hunt divine corruption, even if that meant hunting future incarnations of themselves. They allowed history to remember the La Pelu as evil because fear was safer than temptation.

Their names faded.

Their souls did not.

## Chapter One — The Temple Under Avaotaon

The [[World/Events/Flaming Retribution|Flaming Retribution]] ended the Third Age in fire.

The Blessed Realm vanished beneath falling stars, boiling water, and the judgment of a god whose prophets had gone unheard. Survivors fled north and built a settlement called [[World/Locations/Avaotaon|Avaotaon]], where tents became homes, grief became work, and every day lived was a refusal to let the old kingdom's death become the only story left.

Four people helped hold that community together.

Alwor Thistlegift was a gnome old enough to have outlived everyone he had once called family. He survived the Bleaching by treating wonder as a duty. He cooked, played music, told stories, and became one of the fathers a broken town borrowed when its own were gone.

Cahir Emersyn was a Dromaar blacksmith with a warrior's temper and a protector's hands. The Retribution had killed his father. His sister Angel kept hope alive in him; his brother Gald kept every old family wound open.

Karsten Kendrick belonged to the Solaris Sentinels, a hidden order within the faith of Raythelion. He had been raised to believe duty was a straight road. The end of the world taught him that roads could crack beneath righteous feet.

Simear was the son of an elven adventurer and a cloud dragon, at home in neither inheritance and useful in almost any workshop. He understood machines more readily than institutions and people more readily than crowds.

They were in Avaotaon's market when the ground disappeared.

The sinkhole swallowed all four without warning. They caught ledges, broken stone, and one another on the way down, landing upon an ancient bridge above a lake of falling sand.

Dead goblins surrounded them.

Some stood up.

So did the corpse of an adventurer whose body had been caught in the broken influence of time. The artifact below had tried to reverse death without restoring the spirit. What rose was flesh remembering motion and nothing else.

Beyond the bridge stood a temple that did not belong beneath Avaotaon. Its stone held desert heat. Sand fell forever without accumulating. The building had not been buried. It had been moved.

The temple was **[[World/Locations/Luakini o ka Manawa|Luakini o ka Manawa]]**, sacred to Shananooah, goddess of time and space. Throughout history it had appeared wherever darkness and light approached a turning point.

Goblins were already inside.

They searched for the Great Time Turner.

## Chapter Two — The Broken Hours

The temple's corridors folded distance and age together. Rooms opened where no room should fit. Undead adventurers wandered beneath sand that had never touched the land above. Goblin warriors sang themselves into courage while something unseen drove them deeper.

At the temple's heart, the four found [[World/Items/Artifacts/The Great Time Turner|the Great Time Turner]] broken.

It was no common hourglass. It was a divine regulator, a machine through which the orderly current of time passed into the world. Its damage bled strangeness into the dead and made the temple slip across Aetheria.

The party prevented the goblins from taking control of it. With star metal and desperate craft, they made a temporary repair.

The effort awakened something in them.

Power appeared first as instinct. Light answered where it had not before. Flame, command, wind, memory, music—gifts associated in every culture with the feared La Pelu. Most people believed La Pelu were corruptions wearing mortal flesh. The four heroes returned to the surface carrying the very signs their neighbors had been taught to burn.

They kept what they could hidden.

Six months passed.

Cahir armed Avaotaon's militia and built defenses while Gald brought reports of organized goblin patrols probing the woods. Karsten searched ancient records for Chronal Prism, the only substance that might repair the Time Turner permanently. Alwor discovered that the magic blooming in his music belonged to a fragment of the Llano. Simear honed his draconic gifts and wondered why memories of a desert chieftain came to him like dreams.

All four began remembering lives they had never lived.

Alwor saw himself in a golden city holding a kingdom together through loyalty and intelligence. Cahir remembered an army of giants, humans, orcs, and elves waiting beneath his command. Karsten remembered the certainty of a general who could read battle like written law. Simear stood beside a chieftain as counselor, brother, and the quiet threat behind peace.

The goblins dug toward the temple.

They believed the Time Turner could erase the Flaming Retribution.

They were not wrong.

They simply did not understand who wanted history changed.

## Chapter Three — The Burned Wood

The second goblin attack struck Avaotaon through tunnels beneath the settlement.

When the fighting ended, villagers and goblins lay dead among damaged homes. The surviving attackers bore red patterns across green skin like cracked earth. They were Fire Walkers—or slaves taken by the Fire Walker tribe and driven by a coven that used dreams and domination as chains.

Jared, the exiled priest who had led many survivors north before the Retribution, gave the old enemy a name.

Vorath the Betrayer.

Clues found in the tunnels spoke of him as the Unmaker of Ages. A prayer promised his return beneath the broken tower of Kuth'Alar. A whispering skull warned that the sands of time would turn again and the flame be snuffed out. Blood-marked runes promised a return to the world before fire fell.

The party followed the northern tunnels into the Burned Wood.

They expected another army.

They found refugees.

The goblins there came from many conquered tribes. The Fire Walkers and their hags had taken their families, broken their leaders, and forced them into attacks they no longer wished to make. Grimfang the Relentless met the heroes prepared to fight long enough for his people to escape.

The encounter did not become a massacre.

Peace was difficult, suspicious, and incomplete, but it existed. The Burned Wood goblins would watch the southern approaches. Avaotaon would stop treating every green face as an extension of Vorath's will.

The trail of the hags led south into what remained of the Blessed Realm.

There, the world boiled.

## Chapter Four — Oloru

The swamp had once been cities and roads. Now archways rose from scalding water, level stones vanished beneath reeds, and the dead walked in uniforms from a kingdom that no longer existed.

The party met Prince Troubadour while he was trying to survive it.

The Tripkee knight spoke in grand declarations that his circumstances did not always support. He was brave, earnest, occasionally absurd, and the son of a people called the Namar'Zul. The Tripkee had followed Uhlvara into the swamp and made their home upon Oloru, a sea turtle so ancient that shorelines had once changed around him.

The party returned Troubadour safely to his family. Chief Croakmar Greenleaf welcomed them with a feast. Lady Lilypad and Frorella celebrated the prince's survival. Only Toadwin, the chief's elder son and finest hunter, remained absent.

[[World/Locations/Oloru|Oloru]] remembered more than any mortal historian.

When Alwor spoke with him, the great turtle told the truth the world had buried: the La Pelu had not all been evil. Most were divine servants. Some had been corrupted by Erabose, and the crimes of those few had poisoned the name of all.

The heroes' impossible memories were not curses appearing for the first time.

They were inheritances returning.

Beneath a boiling lake near Oloru lay the [[World/Locations/The Blessed Realm/Sunken Temple of Cadens|Sunken Temple of Cadens]]. If any hidden sanctuary still held a verse of the Llano, it would be there.

Cahir reverse-engineered a potion of fire resistance. Simear helped design a submersible paddleboat sealed in protective tar. Alwor created the illusion of a corpse far out in the lake, drawing away the brine sharks. Karsten drove the vessel toward the drowned temple before the predators understood the trick.

Inside, glyphs flashed through the flooded corridor in a rhythm only Alwor could hear. He guided the submersible through them and into an air pocket beneath a mirrored dome.

The moment his foot touched the marble, he remembered hiding a sword there in another life.

One by one, the others stepped onto the floor.

One by one, they remembered.

## Chapter Five — The Veilcant Vault

Alwor remembered singing the last notes of the Llano while sealing the Beacon's Blade into stone.

Cahir remembered removing an orichalcum crown—the Heart of the Flame—and saying that kings must burn away before light could rise again.

Karsten remembered binding the Infernal Breeze Gauntlets into stasis because hands that had broken armies were needed for peace.

Simear remembered placing the Mirror of Ashes beneath water after entrusting it with the truths the world was not ready to know.

They found Argail, Herald of Cadens, waiting in a composer's study as if their absence had lasted only an afternoon.

Their relics lay beyond a sealed door. To open it, each hero had to reverse the vow once used to hide them.

Alwor declared that he believed the world—or at least he himself—was ready.

Simear said that what had been forgotten must be restored.

Cahir called for the kings to rise again.

Karsten promised that the gauntlets that once broke armies would now bring unity and peace.

The vault opened, returning [[World/Items/Artifacts/Beacon's Blade|Beacon's Blade]], the [[World/Items/Artifacts/Heart of the Flame|Heart of the Flame]], the [[World/Items/Artifacts/Infernal Breeze Gauntlets|Infernal Breeze Gauntlets]], and the [[World/Items/Artifacts/Mirror of Ashes|Mirror of Ashes]].

Stone yielded the Beacon's Blade to Alwor. A molten pillar reformed the Heart of the Flame for Cahir. Simear drew the Mirror of Ashes from a basin with a perfect mirrored surface. Karsten lifted the gauntlets from a statue that had once worn his face and no longer did.

When the fourth relic was claimed, the hidden Llano scroll fell from its beam of light.

The Veilcant Guard had returned without yet remembering all it had done.

They knew enough to understand why the world would fear them.

They also knew who would be waiting for them to become powerful.

## Chapter Six — Velkra's Dream

Toadwin had been taken by the hag coven.

The party followed the hunters' trail through swamp and nightmare to a cave where Velkra waited with her sisters and a nightmare horse. The hags had served the Fire Walkers, guided goblin attacks, and built an Ember Key that required royal blood.

The fight reached into thought as often as flesh. Sleep dragged Karsten toward the ground. Domination pressed against Cahir's will. Alwor used a true name and slowed Velkra's movement through the world. Calm emotions froze moments of violence long enough for the party to survive them.

Simear ended the nightmare hag with a psychic blow that crushed the last shape of her resistance.

The nightmare horse fled before anyone could convince it that its employment had ended.

Toadwin hung bound above a cauldron of golden liquid. The heroes cut him down and found in him a quieter strength than his younger brother's theatrical courage. He had been attacked by goblins and delivered to the coven without knowing why royal blood mattered.

Velkra's diary supplied the answer.

Vorath had directed her.

He had pursued a plan for years, perhaps ages, until an unknown “fool” disrupted it. Now he intended to use Luakini o ka Manawa to reverse time and undo the mistake. The Ember Key would open the path. The goblins, hags, temple, Chronal Prism, and Time Turner were parts of one attempt to restore a future that had already failed.

The party also found a black sphere.

It held Velkra's fear, reverence, and longing for its true master. As the heroes left the cave, the sphere flashed.

A voice entered every mind.

*You will not get in my way again. I will stop you this time before you become powerful enough to defeat me.*

Then the world changed.

## Chapter Seven — The Featherbed Inn

They woke in [[World/Locations/Broadhurst|Broadhurst]].

Not Alwor, Cahir, Karsten, and Simear.

Aurora opened her eyes with telepathy already brushing the edges of her companions' thoughts. Kroff woke angry at his shorter dwarven body and the fading memory of having once been larger. Duncan knelt in contemplation, a paladin weighing the meaning of judgment across lifetimes. Poe woke small, fiery, hungry, and delighted by the possibility that breakfast and violence might both be near.

They knew one another.

They knew they were incarnations of the Veilcant Guard.

They also knew that the Order of the Dawnguard and the Sentinels of Equilibrium hunted them as dangerous La Pelu. The very institutions created after the ancient war now pursued the souls they had been founded to contain.

The previous life remained as a shared dream: Velkra's cave, the black sphere, and Vorath's promise to act earlier this time.

The four fugitives were traveling toward Luminopolis and trying not to attract notice.

By midday they had agreed to rescue a kidnapped child.

Ashley, a blacksmith in Broadhurst, had begged the local Dawnguard for help. Goblins had taken her daughter Violet into the haunted Tomb of Delius in the Silverwood. The Dawnguard had more important matters.

Aurora promised that they did not.

The entrance to the [[World/Locations/Tomb of Delius|Tomb of Delius]] lay an hour from town beneath moss and old stone. Goblins emerged before the party reached it. Poe opened with fire. Kroff turned every wall into a weapon by throwing enemies against it. Aurora moved opponents and allies across the battlefield with invisible force. Duncan judged the creatures one by one and gave his own strength to keep Poe standing.

More goblins came from the tomb.

The fugitives went inside.

## Chapter Eight — The Tomb of Delius

The goblins had made a fortress of the first chamber.

A central bonfire lit spear captains, axe warriors, and crossbow teams. They expected trouble, but not heroes who fought as if some older memory still arranged them into formation.

Duncan drew a captain into the center of the party's strength. Kroff knocked goblins into flame and stone. Poe stood where a sensible mage would not and burned two leaders out of the fight. Aurora turned distance and collision into weapons.

The goblins pushed Duncan into the bonfire again and again. Crossbow bolts found Poe and Aurora. Still, judgment passed from dead foe to living foe until every action the enemy took seemed to condemn another of them.

Poe ended the battle with Conflagration. Aurora reached through memory and gave him the moment again. The second eruption erased the remaining snipers.

Deeper in the tomb, the party found Violet's lunch wrapped in a handkerchief.

They also found an oversized staff carved with dark sorcery runes. Goblins had not come to Delius alone.

Frescoes lined the next corridor: fallen knights carrying a silver chalice through an old war. Kroff found the pressure plate before the scythes found them and wedged the trap open with the confidence of someone who considered ancient murder mechanisms a form of poor smithing.

The burial chamber beyond held twelve sarcophagi.

A red skull glowed upon the floor. At the northern wall, a statue of a human warrior stood above an inscription:

*Once this fails, the battle is lost.*

Chanting sounded behind the stone.

Poe approached the coffins.

All twelve opened.

Zombies, skeletons, and ghouls rose together, every dead face turning toward the small elementalist at the center of the room. Somewhere beyond the wall, the ritual continued. Somewhere deeper in the tomb, Violet waited. Somewhere beyond a single lifetime, Vorath was already changing his plan.

The Veilcant Guard raised their weapons.

The dawn had remembered them.

Now the dark had too.
