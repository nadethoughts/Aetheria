# Nimble Session 06 Report

## Executive Summary

Session 06 began by resolving the griffin attack that ended Session 05. What initially looked like a straightforward predator encounter became the party's introduction to the larger war developing in the Balshar Mountains. The party badly wounded—but did not kill—the griffin. When six more griffins appeared carrying blue-clad orc riders, the wounded animal backed down and indicated that the newcomers were its allies.

The riders belonged to the Cloud Seer orcs. Their leader, **Zing Zow**, was revealed to be an old childhood friend of **Lil Chang**. Zing Zow explained that the Cloud Seers had answered a call for aid from the Erie, but had arrived too late to save the destroyed outpost. He then delivered the session's central revelation: a dragon named **Razor** has moved into the southern mountains, gathered nearly all of the local kobolds and the Black Fang orcs, and begun a campaign to dominate the region.

Razor's campaign is especially dangerous because the Erie guard both powerful anti-dragon artifacts and a nearby mine containing ore that can strengthen a dragon's breath weapon. The Erie war effort is being directed by **General Brand** from the Erie city, while the Cloud Seers have allied with the Erie against Razor. Other orc tribes remain uncommitted and are waiting to see which side will win.

The party considered several responses: secure the mine, go to the Erie capital, recruit the unaligned orc tribes, or somehow confront Razor. Zing Zow and his riders volunteered to secure the mine and call for reinforcements. Directly attacking Razor was ruled out because the dragon is far beyond the party's present strength. By the end of the session, the party's stated next objective was to persuade the remaining orc tribes to join the Erie–Cloud Seer alliance.

The session also made Umbrail's history immediately relevant. The table established that Umbrail's dead mentor had carried one of the Erie's lesser artifacts, and that Umbrail unknowingly took it when he left home. Umbrail therefore may be carrying one of the objects Razor is seeking. Returning it—or deciding who can safely receive it—became an important unresolved personal and strategic problem.

Finally, the party advanced to **level 3** and discussed subclasses and new abilities. The session ended before the party selected a specific tribe, route, or diplomatic approach for its new mission.

## Detailed Chronological Report

### 1. The Griffin Fight Resumed

The session opened in the middle of the encounter foreshadowed at the end of Session 05. The griffin was airborne near the mountain edge, making it difficult for the party's melee combatants to reach it.

The party first tried to understand and control the encounter rather than merely trade attacks:

- The group recalled that griffins are more common in hills and fields than in high mountains, although their presence in the region is not unheard of.
- A character used lore to identify the griffin's wing joint as a vulnerable point, creating an opening for a later attack.
- Rowan deliberately drew the griffin's attention, using his size, confidence, and fearlessness to create an opening for an ally.
- Umbrail used **True Strike** to support allies who could capitalize on the opening.

Umbrail then used the **Flamecaller's Talisman** to make the griffin Smoldering. The talisman failed its depletion roll and was immediately destroyed. Umbrail nevertheless converted the brief condition into a strong **Ignite**, dealing 22 damage and setting the griffin ablaze in midair.

Rubius moved twice to get within throwing distance and hurled his shadow axe at the griffin's wing. The axe grazed the creature and went over the edge. The axe was present with Rubius again later in the session, although the transcript does not narrate how it returned or was recovered.

The griffin then dove on Lil Chang. Rowan used **Interpose** and **Defend**, taking the attack and becoming grappled while reducing what would have been a severe hit to a much smaller amount of damage. Alder commanded his shadow minions to attack and followed with Shadow Blast. The concentrated retaliation convinced the wounded griffin to release Rowan and retreat rather than continue fighting.

### 2. The Encounter Changed from Combat to Parley

As the wounded griffin backed away, the party noticed approximately six more griffins approaching, each carrying an orc rider. The newcomers looked like an organized military patrol rather than another group of wild predators.

The characters reacted cautiously:

- Lil Chang tried to question the wounded griffin.
- Rowan took a defensive posture and assessed the riders for the broken-fang markers seen on the hostile orc bodies from the previous session. He saw none.
- Umbrail tried to read the incoming formation and prepare the party against an attack.
- Rubius, despite still being enraged, used Nature Craft to interpret the griffin's body language. He concluded that it regarded Umbrail as an ally and Lil Chang as a threat.
- Alder remained low among the bodies and used them as cover.

Once Umbrail approached, the wounded griffin allowed him near and communicated in rough griffin speech. It indicated that the approaching riders were its friends and that they would explain the situation. This gave the party enough reason to hold its fire.

The griffin remained wary of the party members who had attacked it. An offer to treat its wounds was rejected, and it characterized one of the attackers as a “bad man.” The creature survived the encounter, but its exact status after the riders arrived was not explicitly resolved.

### 3. The Cloud Seer Orcs Arrived

The riders wore woven tunics in pale and royal blue rather than the usual hides associated with many local orc groups. Their war paint used similar blue tones in swirling patterns. Their leader dismounted cautiously, kept a hand near his axe, and studied every party member before recognizing Lil Chang.

The leader was named **Zing Zow**, a member of the **Cloud Seer tribe** and a childhood friend of Lil Chang. The recognition established an immediate personal connection between the party and the arriving patrol. Lil Chang's precise history with Zing Zow and the Cloud Seers was left flexible, but the relationship was warm enough to begin a conversation instead of a confrontation.

Zing Zow explained that the Cloud Seers had been responding to the Erie's call for aid. They knew the outpost was under attack but arrived too late to intervene. Their alliance with the Erie was based on necessity: both groups faced a much larger common enemy.

### 4. Razor and the Mountain War Were Revealed

Zing Zow reported that a dragon named **Razor** had come into the Balshar Mountains from the hill country to the southwest. Razor has established a roost in the southern mountains and is gathering an army.

The political situation described at the table was:

- **The Erie and Cloud Seer orcs** are allied against Razor.
- **The Black Fang orcs** have allied with Razor, apparently as a matter of convenience rather than devotion.
- **Nearly all of the mountain kobolds** rapidly joined Razor and treat the dragon almost like a god. The kobolds encountered earlier by the party were probably deserters or outliers.
- **Several other orc tribes** remain uncommitted. They are waiting to see which side gains the advantage.

Razor is not simply raiding. He appears to be seeking a permanent foothold and the power to control the mountains. Zing Zow warned that a dragon's ambition and greed rarely stop once conquest begins; if Razor secures the mountains, expansion beyond them is likely.

The party briefly considered the possibility of fighting Razor. The GM clarified that Razor is a campaign-scale threat, roughly comparable to a level-20 monster, and not an enemy the present party can defeat through normal combat. Multiple bands of warriors, including mixed birdfolk and orc forces, have already gone to face the dragon and never returned.

### 5. The Erie Mine Became an Immediate Objective

Zing Zow revealed that a mine roughly thirty minutes away from the outpost was strategically important to the Erie. Umbrail knew the broad reason even though he did not know every secret: the mine contains a special ore capable of empowering a dragon's breath weapon.

If Razor gains access to that ore, the balance of the war could shift decisively. Umbrail described keeping the mine from Razor as critical—possibly important enough that losing it could end the war before a wider resistance forms.

Zing Zow volunteered to take his riders to secure the mine, assuming it had not already fallen, and to send word to the Cloud Seer leadership for more troops. This delegated the party's most immediate military problem to an allied force, freeing the player characters to handle work for which outsiders might be better suited: recruiting the neutral orc tribes.

The mine's actual condition was not discovered during the session, so Zing Zow's mission remains unresolved off-screen.

### 6. The Erie Artifacts Were Explained

Umbrail's people protect several artifacts connected to the old wars against dragons. They are deliberately distributed rather than stored together:

- The greatest artifact is kept in the Erie capital or principal city.
- Another is hidden in an underground, bunker-like vault at the ancient battlefield where the decisive blow of the Dragon War was struck.
- A further artifact is kept on an eastern Balshar peak associated with the resting place of the Erie general who defeated the dragons.
- Lesser objects of power were entrusted to significant Erie individuals.

During this discussion, Umbrail's player and the GM established an important piece of character history: Umbrail's dead mentor had carried one of the lesser artifacts. When Umbrail left home, he took possessions from his mentor without understanding that one of them was an object of power. Umbrail is therefore probably carrying a target Razor would want.

The object's exact form, powers, limitations, and present location among Umbrail's belongings were not defined. Umbrail also does not yet know the proper authority to whom it should be returned, although the party considered taking it to someone in charge in the Erie city.

### 7. General Brand and the Erie War Effort

The Erie resistance is being led by **General Brand** from the Erie city, described as the people's greatest fortress. Brand does not remain there continuously. He travels often to the eastern Balshar Mountains, sometimes for weeks, and appears to make a repeated circuit through the region.

The reason for Brand's circuit was not explained. It may involve inspecting defenses, maintaining the scattered artifact sites, coordinating forces, or some other purpose not yet revealed.

### 8. The Party Chose a Strategic Direction

The party identified three broad tasks:

1. Convince the unaligned orc tribes to join the resistance.
2. Keep Razor from gaining access to the mine.
3. Get Umbrail and his artifact to an appropriate Erie authority.

There was disagreement over ordering. Going directly to the Erie capital would address Umbrail's artifact and connect the party with General Brand. Going south toward Razor was rejected as suicidal. Retreating or returning home was raised pragmatically, especially by the party members who saw little they could personally do against a dragon.

The coalition plan won support because the Cloud Seers' attempts to recruit other orcs have been obstructed by old feuds. The party may succeed specifically because it is an outside group without generations of local grievances. Returning to the Erie with additional allies could also improve Umbrail's difficult homecoming.

At the final check-in, the party's stated plan was to focus on bringing the other orc tribes into alliance with the Erie. This was a strategic commitment, not a complete itinerary: no first tribe, route, leverage, or diplomatic offer was selected.

### 9. The Party Reached Level 3

The party performed level-up bookkeeping, including hit-point increases, a new skill point, and subclass selection. The conversation indicates the following choices or strong leanings, though the character sheets should be treated as the final authority:

- **Alder:** Eldritch Mushroom was named, along with the ability to maintain an additional and more independent shadow minion.
- **Umbrail:** Invoker of Chaos was discussed, including voluntarily reducing a spell's mana cost and triggering the GM's Chaos Table on a critical result. Umbrail also discussed access to cold magic.
- **Rubius:** Mountain Heart was discussed as the thematic choice over Red Mist, with improved fury-based damage reduction and limited wound recovery.
- **Lil Chang:** Way of Pain was discussed, allowing him to turn a melee hit against him into a critical hit, reduce the damage, and reflect damage to the attacker.
- **Rowan:** Refuge appeared to be the preferred path over Vengeance, expanding his protective aura and Interpose reach.
- **Whisper:** No clear subclass decision appears in the transcript.

Some of this discussion was exploratory rules talk, so any unrecorded sheet updates still need confirmation.

## Character-by-Character Impact

### Umbrail

This was the most consequential session for Umbrail. The regional war transformed his reluctant homecoming from a personal problem into a strategic obligation. The destruction of the outpost, the death of his mentor, the discovery that he carries an Erie artifact, and the prospect of returning with allied orc tribes all pull him back toward the people he left. He now has reasons both to seek General Brand and to avoid leading Razor directly to the object he carries.

### Lil Chang

Lil Chang's past became a diplomatic asset when Zing Zow recognized him. His Cloud Seer connection may give the party credibility with other orcs, but it also raises questions about why he left and what obligations remain. He shifted rapidly from being perceived by the wounded griffin as a threat to being the party member with the strongest personal bond to the newcomers.

### Rowan Hearthboro

Rowan's protective identity was reinforced mechanically and narratively. He drew the griffin's attention, Interposed when it attacked Lil Chang, and helped the party avoid mistaking the Cloud Seers for the same faction as the broken-fang orcs. The apparent Refuge subclass choice deepens his role as the party's defender and could also make him a strong diplomatic representative of the coalition's protective purpose.

### Rubius Haggard

Rubius's rage and shadow axe remained both useful and troubling. He threw the weapon at the griffin, later calmed himself enough to understand the animal's body language, and continued to regard the axe as beneficial. The player wants an eventual path to remove it, but Rubius lacks an in-character reason to reject it yet. Mountain Heart would reinforce his resilience and his giant-related identity.

### Alder

Alder contributed heavily to ending the griffin fight without killing it: his minions and Shadow Blast forced the creature to release Rowan and back down. His pragmatic reaction to the dragon threat was to question whether the party should simply leave or seek safety underground. That caution is a useful counterweight to heroic overcommitment. His new shadow abilities also continue the contrast between his creepy magic and practical temperament.

### Whisper

Whisper did not receive a clearly identifiable spotlight moment in the transcript. It is unclear whether the player was absent, quiet, or lost in transcription. His level-3 advancement and view of the party's new mission remain unrecorded.

## Overall Assessment

Session 06 functioned as the true beginning of the campaign's mountain-war arc. The griffin fight provided immediate danger, but its real purpose was to introduce the Cloud Seers and turn the ruined outpost from an isolated tragedy into evidence of a regional invasion. The party learned that the kobolds and hostile orcs were parts of a larger coalition, gained a credible ally, and found a meaningful role that does not depend on defeating an impossibly powerful dragon in combat.

The strongest narrative development was the integration of existing character histories into the strategic plot. Lil Chang's childhood connection opened the door to the Cloud Seers. Umbrail's mentor and departure from home became tied to the artifacts Razor is hunting. Rubius's shadow axe and Alder's shadow magic remain parallel dangers beneath the more visible dragon crisis. Rowan's protective identity gives the coalition effort a moral center, while Alder's skepticism keeps the group attentive to the difference between brave action and futile sacrifice.

The session ended with a strong campaign direction but deliberately incomplete operational details. The next game needs to turn “recruit the orc tribes” into a concrete mission: identify a tribe, understand its grievance or need, decide what the party can promise, and determine how the Erie and Cloud Seers will honor that promise.
