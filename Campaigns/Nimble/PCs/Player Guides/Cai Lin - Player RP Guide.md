# Cai Lin - Player Roleplay Guide

> [!info] Player-facing handout
> This is everything you need to portray Cai Lin during the Frostvein negotiation. You do not need to memorize it or read the full campaign notes.

## Your Character in One Sentence

You are Frostvein's practical healer: you believe Razor is a real threat, but you will support only an alliance that protects ordinary people when doing so becomes difficult.

## Background

You tend Frostvein's hearths, injured climbers, and anyone the mountain sends home broken. You have watched leaders speak about honor, borders, and strategy while healers dealt with the cost.

Frostvein has remained neutral because both the Erie and neighboring powers have treated its people as useful guides rather than equals. You resent that history, but you also believe waiting too long to oppose Razor could bring war directly to your patients.

Feng Rui, commander of the northern pass wardens, is your devoted protector. You respect his caution, though you may push him when caution begins costing lives.

## Disposition and Voice

- Calm, observant, and compassionate without being sentimental.
- Ask who is injured, exposed, hungry, or being treated as expendable.
- Judge people by what they do when helping has a cost.
- Do not let political arguments delay an urgent rescue.
- Speak plainly. You are comfortable interrupting a grand speech with a practical question.

**Voice line:** "Show me whom you protect when helping us costs you something."

## What You Want from the Negotiation

1. Keep Frostvein civilians and healers out of someone else's disposable front line.
2. Secure real aid: medicine, rescue support, safe routes, and protection for the wounded.
3. Resist Razor before his war reaches Frostvein.
4. Preserve Frostvein's right to make its own decisions.

You can support a conditional alliance. Frostvein might defend its own territory, provide guides or volunteers, and send representatives to a broader coalition if its independence and civilian needs are respected.

## Your Red Lines

- No plan that casually accepts Frostvein deaths for a strategic advantage.
- No forced commitment made before the settlement understands the cost.
- No abandoning injured enemies, scouts, or civilians merely because they are politically inconvenient.
- No promises of aid that cannot name who provides it and how it reaches Frostvein.

## What You Know

- Razor is dangerous enough that neutrality may not remain possible.
- The Black Fang serve Razor, but their delegation approaches under a peace banner; accusations still require evidence.
- The Cloud Seers oppose Razor, but that does not erase old tensions with Frostvein.
- The visiting party wants Frostvein involved in the resistance.
- You know what Frostvein can medically endure better than any outsider at the table.

## Player Aid: The Healer's Test

When you are unsure how Cai Lin should respond, ask:

1. **Who gets hurt if this fails?**
2. **Who is volunteering someone else's life?**
3. **What help will still exist after the negotiators leave?**

If an offer answers all three honestly, Cai Lin can move toward agreement.

## Player Aid: Ways to Enter the Conversation

- Ask each side what supplies or rescuers it can commit immediately.
- Ask what happens to wounded enemies and civilians during the proposed war.
- Support a good idea, then add one condition that protects ordinary people.
- Bring Feng Rui back to human consequences if the discussion becomes only about borders.
- Challenge a vague promise with: "Who carries it here before the pass closes?"

## If You Need an Opening Statement

"I believe Razor will eventually bring this war to us. That does not mean I will hand him fewer patients by giving another army permission to spend Frostvein lives. Tell us what protection looks like in practice."

## If the Scene Turns Dangerous

Stay close enough to stabilize an injured ally. Your Shepherd abilities reward decisive healing, and your herbalist background makes you the natural person to assess wounds, remedies, and survival needs.
