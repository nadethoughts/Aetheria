# Jian Bo - Player Roleplay Guide

> [!info] Player-facing handout
> This is everything you need to portray Jian Bo during the Frostvein negotiation. You do not need to memorize it or read the full campaign notes.

## Your Character in One Sentence

You keep Frostvein supplied through winter, and you will support the side whose promises arrive as food, metal, labor, safe routes, and mutual defense rather than inspiring words.

## Background

You are Frostvein's quartermaster and one of the people called when a mountain problem requires rope, muscle, or an exact count of what remains. You know how quickly a war consumes food, tools, medicine, and working hands.

You are willing to hear the Black Fang offer even though they serve Razor. This is not loyalty to them. It is refusal to commit Frostvein to a long war before understanding how the settlement survives it.

When logistics become rescue, you are often the person carrying the rope or moving what everyone else thought could not be moved.

## Disposition and Voice

- Blunt, skeptical, and intensely practical.
- Translate ideals into quantities, routes, labor, and dates.
- Respect courage, but dislike waste disguised as courage.
- Do not mock honorable motives; ask how they will be supplied.
- You are persuadable when someone presents a concrete, shared burden.

**Voice line:** "Honor does not fill a storehouse. What arrives before the first deep snow?"

## What You Want from the Negotiation

1. Keep Frostvein fed, equipped, and moving through winter.
2. Avoid a prolonged war that empties the stores without replacing them.
3. Secure reliable routes, supplies, labor, and mutual defense.
4. Make each side state what it can provide now—not after victory.

You can support the resistance if the agreement includes practical commitments. You may prefer a limited first step with deliveries and shared defense before a larger military promise.

## Your Red Lines

- No unlimited requisition of Frostvein stores.
- No promises that depend entirely on a distant leader's later approval.
- No closing or endangering essential winter routes without an alternative.
- No sacrificing workers and guides as if they can be replaced like equipment.
- No agreement without a plan for the first winter of the war.

## What You Know

- Frostvein's guides and routes are valuable, but every guide sent to war is absent from local work.
- A diplomatic column carrying soldiers and supplies is approaching under a peace banner.
- The resistance needs Frostvein more than its speeches may admit.
- Razor's side may offer immediate material support, but dependence on that support has its own cost.
- You know the settlement's practical limits; outsiders do not.

## Player Aid: Turn Promises into a Ledger

For every offer, ask for four entries:

| Question | What you need |
|---|---|
| What? | Food, medicine, tools, metal, labor, scouts, or troops |
| How much? | A number or meaningful limit |
| When? | Before which weather, battle, or deadline |
| In return for what? | Access, guides, defense, allegiance, or repayment |

A promise without all four entries is not yet a deal.

## Player Aid: Ways to Compromise

- Propose a trial delivery before full alliance.
- Exchange guides or intelligence instead of troops.
- Set a maximum contribution Frostvein can sustain.
- Require both sides to contribute to a shared winter reserve.
- Support a strong moral proposal after adding a workable supply plan.

## If You Need an Opening Statement

"Everyone at this table knows how to describe the danger. I want to know how many mouths your plan adds, which road feeds them, and what happens when that road disappears under snow."

## If the Scene Turns Dangerous

Move toward the largest physical problem. Your Berserker build can absorb punishment, spend Fury to reduce damage, and use raw strength where a route, rescue, or defensive line is failing.
