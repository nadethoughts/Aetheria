# Nimble Session 04 Report

## Executive Summary

Session 04 dealt with the consequences of Snaggletooth's defeat, returned the stolen livestock to Clackston, and launched the campaign's journey into the Balshar Mountains. The party first explored the boss chamber and found 120 gold hidden inside the ancient statue. They debated whether to bury Snaggletooth's shadow axe with the ruin. After initially placing it in the statue's empty hand, Rubius chose to touch and claim it.

The axe resized itself for Rubius and flooded him with a feeling of peace, friendship, and safety. It then spoke inside his mind: **"Welcome, friend. Let's drink the light together."** Although the rest of the party recognized the weapon as the likely object named in the entrance warning, Rubius perceived no danger. Mechanically, the **Axe of Snaggletooth** became a powerful battleaxe dealing 3d4 damage rather than the usual 1d8.

The group led the recovered animals back to Clackston. Only a small portion of the community's livestock had been lost, so the town retained enough to survive winter. Master Glenson paid the promised 25 gold per character plus the 5-gold early-completion bonus. After shopping, resting, and confirming level-2 advancement, the party chose to investigate why non-native kobolds had appeared near Clackston. Umbrail directed them toward the Balshar Mountains, where kobold groups and his own Birdfolk people were known to live.

Several days into the mountain route, a fur-clad orc blocked the pass and ordered the party to stop. Rowan heard additional creatures behind the boulders, exposing the greeting as an ambush. The ensuing battle pitted the party against several orcs, large boars, and a heavily armed orc leader. Coordinated Help actions, True Strike, shadow minions, protective reactions, and focused attacks allowed the party to win.

Among the dead, the group recovered a **Flamecaller's Talisman**, a consumable item that can apply Smoldering to several targets but may disintegrate each time it is used. The session ended before finalizing its long-term holder. The party was now committed to the mountain investigation, carrying both a suspicious shadow artifact and its first evidence of organized hostility in the region.

- **Major item decision:** Rubius claimed the Axe of Snaggletooth despite the ancient warning.

## Detailed Chronological Report

### 1. The Party Reflected on Its Greatest Regrets

The session opened with a character-development question that added long-term hooks:

- **Alder** regretted losing a family heirloom that accelerated rot and aided food production. It had been entrusted to him when he left to establish his homestead.
- **Whisper** regretted running away from home after stealing something tied to his family. The escape taught him to cheat and survive, but also left him lonely and unsure whether he could return.
- **Rubius** regretted losing a promising ruin. While following evidence connected to the lost giant enclave, he lost control or damaged key supports, collapsing a chamber and burying information that might have helped him or others.
- **Umbrail** believed leaving his dwindling Birdfolk people was the right decision, yet regretted abandoning people who might need him. He feared that his absence could contribute to their extinction.
- **Rowan** regretted waiting so long to leave home. He believed he might have helped more people and experienced more of the world had he departed before the cataclysmic changes affecting Aetheria.

Lil Chang's answer was not clearly captured as part of the same sequence, so the report does not invent one.

### 2. The Boss Chamber Was Searched

The party examined the long chamber, its damaged columns, and the statue holding a shield with an open second hand. Alder discovered a concealed drawer in the statue's base containing **120 gold pieces**. With six active party members, the money was divided evenly into **20 gold each**.

The chamber remained unstable because of the kobolds' excavation. The group discussed collapsing the room to bury the dangerous weapon and prevent anyone from repeating Snaggletooth's ascension. They also recognized the historical loss involved in destroying a Second Age site.

Three of the four kobold workers had escaped, meaning knowledge of the ruin and the party's victory could spread even if the chamber were sealed.

### 3. The Axe Was Returned to the Statue

Because the statue's empty hand appeared made to hold a weapon, a party member lifted the axe through protective fabric and placed it there. Nothing visibly happened. The statue did not animate, the ruin did not activate, and the axe remained inert.

This result strengthened the argument for leaving the weapon behind and collapsing the chamber. Rowan favored burying it and never speaking of it again. Others worried that an intact artifact would eventually be recovered by someone less cautious.

### 4. Rubius Claimed the Axe

The debate ended when Rubius chose to touch the weapon directly. The axe resized itself to suit his Half-Giant frame. He experienced an overwhelming sense of calm, safety, and companionship rather than pain or corruption.

The weapon spoke privately to him:

> Welcome, friend. Let's drink the light together.

This confirmed that the axe was intelligent or carried an intelligent influence. It also strongly suggested that it was the "thing that drinks the light" named in the entrance warning.

The party saw an obviously dangerous shadow artifact. Rubius experienced a friendly, useful weapon that had chosen him. That difference in perception created an unresolved trust problem: Rubius had no in-character reason to discard something that made him feel safe, while his companions had every reason to fear what it might eventually demand.

The item was recorded as the **Axe of Snaggletooth**, a versatile battleaxe dealing **3d4 damage** rather than **1d8**.

### 5. The Livestock Returned to Clackston

With the magical barrier disabled, the party gathered the sheep, goats, mule, and other animals and escorted them back to town. Approximately five percent of Clackston's livestock had been lost, but the recovery prevented the thefts from becoming a winter famine.

Master Glenson honored the original agreement:

- **25 gold per character** for resolving the thefts
- **5 additional gold per character** for finishing within one week

Combined with the 20-gold statue share, each character gained **50 gold during the session** before accounting for personal purchases.

### 6. Recovery, Shopping, and Level Confirmation

The party took a safe rest in Clackston and confirmed the level-2 improvements introduced during the Snaggletooth fight. The players discussed hit points, skill points, equipment, armor, camping supplies, healing resources, and spellcasting tools.

Umbrail acquired a wand associated with **True Strike**. Other purchases were discussed rapidly and are difficult to attribute with confidence; individual character sheets should remain the final authority for armor, weapons, and remaining gold.

### 7. The Party Chose the Balshar Mountains

With Clackston safe, the group debated its next purpose. The black-scaled kobolds were not known as a native local population. Their presence, organization, and shadow weapon suggested that something had pushed or drawn them from elsewhere.

The party decided to investigate the kobolds' origin rather than simply collect payment and separate. Umbrail knew that kobolds and other dangerous groups lived in the high country, so the **Balshar Mountains** became the most plausible direction.

This choice also brought Umbrail closer to the Birdfolk home he had deliberately avoided. At this point he did not commit to visiting his people; he simply served as the party's best regional guide.

### 8. The Mountain-Pass Ambush

After several days of travel, the party entered a narrow pass. A fur-clad orc stepped into view and ordered them to stop. Rowan heard snuffling behind nearby boulders, revealing hidden animals and additional attackers.

The party refused the demand and combat began. The enemy force included:

- Several orc raiders
- Large war boars
- A larger orc wielding two battleaxes

The encounter emphasized combined tactics. Whisper moved aggressively between targets, Alder summoned multiple shadow minions, Umbrail used Frost Shield and support magic, Rowan stacked True Strike and Help effects while protecting the line, Lil Chang exploited mobility, and Rubius absorbed punishment while using the new axe.

The Help and Create an Opening rules became especially important. Players repeatedly generated advantage for the next attacker instead of treating every action as a separate damage attempt.

### 9. The Flamecaller's Talisman

After defeating the ambushers, the party searched the bodies. One orc carried a **Flamecaller's Talisman**.

The item requires an action and can apply **Smoldering** to a number of creatures based on the user's key attribute. Each use requires a d4 roll; on a 1, the talisman disintegrates into ash. Smoldering increases the effectiveness of fire magic and can interact with other class abilities.

The group recognized that Umbrail could exploit the condition, but it might be more efficient for a character with fewer competing actions to activate the talisman for him. The session ended without a completely clear permanent assignment. Session 05 resumed this discussion.

## Character-by-Character Impact

### Rubius Haggard

Rubius made the session's most consequential choice. The axe offered both mechanical power and emotional comfort, targeting his desire for friendship and acceptance rather than threatening him openly. His previous regret about destroying a ruin also made burying another source of history psychologically complicated. He now carries an artifact that his companions fear but that he experiences as benevolent.

### Umbrail

Umbrail's regret made the decision to enter the Balshar Mountains personally difficult. He served as the party's regional guide while continuing to avoid a direct homecoming. His fire magic also made the Flamecaller's Talisman strategically relevant to him.

### Rowan Hearthboro

Rowan argued for caution around the axe and reinforced his role as the party's protective conscience. During the pass encounter, his awareness exposed the ambush before it fully closed and his support actions helped allies land decisive attacks.

### Alder

Alder's lost heirloom created a concrete future quest. In combat, his multiple shadows absorbed attacks and expanded the party's action economy. The contrast between his controlled shadow magic and the axe's predatory shadow influence became more pronounced.

### Whisper

Whisper's regret revealed loneliness beneath his appetite for excitement. The mountain battle allowed him to move between targets, recover thrown weapons, and use advantage efficiently, reinforcing his role as an opportunistic finisher.

### Lil Chang

Lil Chang was fully integrated into the party during the return to Clackston and subsequent journey. His speed and melee pressure complemented Rowan and Rubius in the confined mountain pass, though his personal history received less attention than the original five characters' regrets.

## Overall Assessment

Session 04 was a transition from the self-contained Clackston adventure into the campaign's mountain arc. Returning the animals provided a complete success and a believable reason for the townspeople to trust the party. Choosing to remain together showed that the characters now had a purpose beyond earning Glenson's reward.

The axe decision gave that victory a dangerous cost. The party removed the object that likely empowered Snaggletooth and brought it into the wider world, where its influence can grow through Rubius. The mountain ambush then demonstrated that the kobolds were only one part of a more complicated regional situation. The session ended with new enemies, a new consumable artifact, and Umbrail moving closer to the people he feared he had abandoned.

## Continuity and Transcript Notes

- The canonical weapon name used here is **Axe of Snaggletooth**. It is also described informally as the shadow axe.
- The phrase **"drink the light"** deliberately echoes the entrance warning and is treated as a direct statement from the weapon or its intelligence.
- The yellow creatures in the mountain-pass encounter are identified in the transcript as **boars** or "war pigs," not wargs. Session 05 contains a separate encounter with giant wolf-like creatures.
- The hostile orcs' broken-fang tokens are discovered explicitly at the start of Session 05, so no formal tribe name is assigned in this report.
- Shopping details are left conservative because the automatic transcript makes several buyers and items difficult to distinguish.
- The Birdfolk homeland and organization are rendered **Erie** in later campaign notes, though the common word "aerie" may be the intended linguistic origin.
