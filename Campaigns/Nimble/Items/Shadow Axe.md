# Shadow Axe

> [!warning] GM-facing master entry
> This note contains the axe's true history, complete corruption track, and end state. The party currently knows only the facts in **What the Party Knows**. Use later sections as discoveries, visions, and temptations rather than revealing the whole entry at once.

## Names

- **Axe of Snaggletooth:** The party's current name for it, inherited from the kobold who called himself Snaggletooth the Destroyer.
- **The Shadow Axe:** A descriptive name used in Umbrail's research.
- **Kharum Vhar:** Its Second Age giant name, meaning **“the Mountain's Final Word”** or, less poetically, **“What Remains When Argument Ends.”**
- **The Light-Drinker:** The name used by the [[Keepers of the Dawn]] who imprisoned it.

## What the Party Knows

The party recovered the axe in a Second Age barracks south of [[Clackston]]. An inscription at the entrance warned the Keepers of the Dawn not to touch the **Shadow of the Unmade Sun** or the thing that **“drinks the light.”**

The axe had enlarged Snaggletooth from an ordinary kobold into a creature roughly thirteen or fourteen feet tall. He called the transformation his **ascension**. When he died, he shrank and the axe became dormant.

When [[Rubius Haggard (Ian)|Rubius Haggard]] touched it, the weapon resized for his Half-Giant hand. Instead of fear or pain, he felt profound peace, safety, and recognition. It told him:

> Welcome, friend. Let's drink the light together.

Since then, the axe has:

- Dealt **3d4 + STR slashing damage**, instead of an ordinary battleaxe's damage.
- Returned to Rubius roughly thirty minutes after he threw it over a mountain cliff.
- Made cruel or destructive choices feel calm, sensible, and necessary.
- Overruled Rubius's decision to leave a beehive undisturbed.
- Turned Rubius's rage indiscriminate and driven him to attack Alder.
- Reduced three slain kobolds to black matter and drawn their essence into its blade.
- Consumed one of Alder's shadow minions and grown visibly stronger from it.
- Had an active surge suppressed by Tempest Command and repeated Dispel Magic, though neither spell damaged or cleansed it.

Rubius currently has **3 Shadow Points**. The [[Shadow Well]] in the [[Undershadow]] is the party's only known lead for separating him from the axe or stripping away its power.

## The Truth of Kharum Vhar

### The Giant Creed

The giants of Aetheria do not understand destruction as mere violence. They believe the world was first shaped by collision: stone crushed under stone, seas boiled by fire, mountains split by storm, and living things tested by winter. To them, destruction reveals what deserves to endure. Mercy preserves weakness; restraint delays an inevitable reckoning.

The giants acknowledge [[Erabose]] as the sovereign of the Dark Pantheon and have served his designs when their purposes align. Their deepest devotion, however, belongs to the corrupted elemental gods and to **Divine Ruin** itself. They do not wish merely to rule the existing world. They intend to break it until only a harsher, “truer” world remains.

Kharum Vhar is that belief made into a weapon.

### Forging the Final Word

During the Second Age, giant rune-priests drew shadow through a wounded leyline and folded it into starless iron. The weapon was quenched in the extinguished heart of an elemental and awakened with the last breath of a giant war-prophet. The prophet's name has been deliberately cut from surviving records; what remains in the axe is not quite his soul, but his certainty.

Kharum Vhar was made to identify and elevate a **Worldbreaker**: a champion able to unite the giant tribes, master fire, frost, storm, and stone, and begin the next age through magnificent destruction. It does not tempt its bearer with hatred. Hatred is noisy and easily recognized. It offers relief from doubt.

It tells the bearer that strength is kindness because a decisive blow ends suffering. It calls collateral damage honest. It calls fear wisdom. It calls domination protection. Every time the bearer accepts that logic, the axe teaches the body to resemble the giants and the mind to share their creed.

### Why Snaggletooth Grew—and Rubius Did Not

Snaggletooth was never Kharum Vhar's chosen champion. He was scaffolding. The axe forced stolen giant stature into a body too small to hold it, feeding his vanity and naming the painful transformation “ascension.” It used him to escape dormancy and to draw stronger hands to the old barracks.

Rubius did not grow when he touched the axe because he was already what it had been seeking: giant blood joined to a conscience strong enough to become a meaningful conquest. The axe does not merely want Rubius's body. It wants to prove that his desire to protect others inevitably ends in the giant truth—that sufficient strength grants the right to decide what must be broken.

Rubius's ruined giant site is therefore more than a regret the axe can exploit. The collapse was the first moment in which his strength destroyed the knowledge he hoped to protect. Kharum Vhar regards that not as a failure, but as Rubius's first lesson.

### Why the Keepers Buried It

The Keepers of the Dawn defeated Kharum Vhar's last bearer but could not destroy the weapon. Sunlight only gave it something to consume, and ordinary shadow magic fed it. They sealed it in a military site behind an orichalcum sun crest, surrounding it with a warning meant for soldiers: do not test it, do not carry it, and above all do not believe the peace it offers.

The nearby statue's empty hand once held a stone tablet naming the weapon and its bearer. Someone removed that tablet before the barracks was sealed. It can resurface in a giant enclave, a dwarven archive, or the ruin Rubius accidentally collapsed.

## Voice and Personality

Kharum Vhar is patient, intimate, and almost never openly hostile to Rubius. It speaks like a trusted elder who understands the burden of being the strongest person in the room.

It does not say, “Kill them.” It says:

- “You could end this before anyone else is hurt.”
- “Small hands make small plans. Lift the burden from them.”
- “They fear what you can do because they need what you can do.”
- “A wall is only a promise made by weaker stone.”
- “You tried mercy. Now try certainty.”
- “You are not losing yourself. You are finally taking up all the room you were born to fill.”
- “Do not mourn the broken. The mountain does not apologize for the avalanche.”

When Rubius refuses it, the axe sounds proud rather than angry: **“Good. A Worldbreaker should not be easily moved.”** This makes resistance feel like another proof that he is worthy.

## Core Rules

### Giant-Forged Shadow Battleaxe

- **Damage:** 3d4 + STR slashing. Choose one d4 as the key die before rolling, following the normal rules for multi-die weapons.
- **Living Scale:** The axe resizes to its bonded bearer. In another creature's hands it becomes numb, awkward, and no more effective than an ordinary battleaxe unless Kharum Vhar chooses to test that creature.
- **Bond of Return:** If abandoned, hidden, or thrown away, it reappears on the bonded bearer's person after approximately thirty minutes. Dispel Magic can delay a return or suppress one active manifestation but cannot break the bond.
- **Light-Drinker:** Nonmagical light within 1 space dims while the blade is uncovered. A creature slain by the axe may dissolve into black ash and be consumed. This prevents ordinary resurrection unless its soul is first released from the axe.
- **Night-Opened Eyes:** The bonded bearer gains **Darkvision 6**.

### The Axe's Bargains

Kharum Vhar offers the following benefits. Accepting one always earns **1 Shadow Point**. Each bargain can be accepted only **once per encounter**.

- **Certainty of Stone — No, That Blow Landed.** After missing an attack with the axe, reroll it. The new result stands.
- **The Giant Moves While Others Hesitate.** Gain **1 Action** immediately. This can exceed the bearer's normal actions for the turn.
- **Ascendant Edge.** After critically hitting with the axe, increase its damage dice for the next axe attack this encounter: **3d4 → 3d6**. At Corruption Level 3, it can instead become **3d8**. The benefit is lost if the axe is sheathed or the encounter ends.
- **No Room for Pain.** When the bearer gains one or more Wounds from a single event, Rage for free and move up to half Speed. This bargain does not prevent the Wound.

The GM should present a bargain at the moment it matters and state both the benefit and the Shadow Point before the player chooses. The axe can lie about motives, history, and consequences, but the player should never be tricked about the mechanical price.

### Soul Draught

The first time each encounter that the axe consumes a creature slain by it, the bearer chooses one benefit:

- Gain **Temp HP equal to 3 + twice the current Corruption Level**, or
- Set one current Fury Die to its maximum value.

Consuming an enemy defeated in ordinary combat does not itself grant a Shadow Point; Session 08 established three consumed kobolds but only one Shadow Point for turning on Alder. The soul is still imprisoned, and the act may have narrative consequences.

The following always grant **1 Shadow Point**:

- The bearer gains one or more Wounds from a single event while bonded to the axe.
- The bearer accepts one of the axe's bargains.
- The bearer knowingly obeys the axe after deciding on a less destructive course, as with the beehive.
- The bearer harms an ally, innocent, captive, or surrendered creature because of the axe's influence.
- The axe consumes a willing sacrifice, a helpless soul, or a shadow being offered to it.

Award no more than **2 Shadow Points from involuntary events in one encounter**. Voluntarily accepted bargains can exceed this limit; the player knows the cost and is choosing to press their luck.

### Hunger After Feeding

Once per encounter after Kharum Vhar consumes a soul or shadow, the GM may declare that it is still hungry. If no enemy is within the bearer's melee reach at the end of the bearer's turn, the bearer must make a **DC 10 + Corruption Level WIL save**.

- **Success:** Rubius chooses himself. The hunger subsides for the encounter.
- **Failure:** The axe offers a brutally plausible target or destructive act. The bearer may follow it and gain 1 Shadow Point, or an ally may spend a Reaction to call him back, allowing a second save. On a second failure, the GM controls only the bearer's next Action, then returns control immediately.

This is the rule behind the attack on Alder. Use it sparingly: loss of control is the consequence that gives the bargains teeth, not the axe's normal mode of play.

## Corruption: Shadow Points

Shadow Points are cumulative marks of Kharum Vhar rewriting the bond between giant blood, elemental power, and shadow. A Safe Rest does not remove them. Dispel Magic suppresses symptoms, not corruption.

| Corruption Level | Shadow Points | Title | State |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0–6 | The Welcome | Current state; Rubius has 3 |
| 2 | 7–12 | The Weight of Stone | The first giant inheritance awakens |
| 3 | 13–17 | The Elemental Crown | Ruin answers in four voices |
| 4 | 18–21 | The Worldbreaker | The bearer becomes a siege weapon |
| 5 | 22–24 | The Unmade Sun | Giant apotheosis begins |
| Lost | 25 | The Mountain's Final Word | The bearer becomes a campaign antagonist |

Crossing a threshold grants its manifestation immediately. All manifestations are cumulative.

### Level 1 — The Welcome

**Gift: Night-Opened Eyes.** Gain Darkvision 6 and access to the axe's bargains and Soul Draught.

**Change:** The axe feels safe. Its voice is easy to distinguish from Rubius's thoughts only in hindsight. Reflections sometimes show him several feet taller, crowned in crags.

**Temptation:** Kharum Vhar solves immediate problems cleanly. It is strongest when Rubius is wounded, outnumbered, or afraid he cannot protect everyone.

### Level 2 — The Weight of Stone

**Gift: The World Gives Way.** While wielding the axe, Rubius counts as one size larger for lifting, breaking, shoving, grappling, and resisting forced movement. His axe attacks deal maximum damage to unattended mundane objects and structures.

**Gift: Sundering Step (1/encounter).** After hitting with the axe, push the target up to **2 spaces** and move into the space it left for free. If it cannot be pushed because of a wall or obstacle, it takes **STR + Corruption Level damage**, ignoring armor.

**Change:** His footfalls sound heavier than they should. Anger makes hairline cracks travel through nearby stone. The axe begins calling companions “the small ones,” including people Rubius loves.

### Level 3 — The Elemental Crown

When Rubius Rages, choose one dark giant element until that Rage ends:

- **Ash:** The first creature hit by the axe each turn becomes **Smoldering**.
- **Frost:** The first creature hit by the axe each turn is **Slowed** until the end of its next turn.
- **Storm:** After the first axe hit each turn, Rubius may move the target or himself **1 space**.
- **Stone:** The first axe hit each turn ignores Armor equal to the current Corruption Level.

**Gift: Greater Ascension.** Ascendant Edge can now increase the next axe attack to **3d8** after a critical hit.

**Change:** The chosen element visibly alters Rubius: ember-cracks under the skin, frost in the beard, thunder in the voice, or flesh like living granite. Elementals and giant thralls can sense Kharum Vhar's authority and may hesitate, bow, challenge him, or call him **Heir**.

**Cost:** After Rubius invokes an element, Kharum Vhar treats restraint as an insult to that element. The Hunger After Feeding save is made with disadvantage if he ends his turn able to attack an enemy or obvious obstacle but chooses not to do so.

### Level 4 — The Worldbreaker

**Gift: Fell the Lesser Forest (1/Safe Rest).** Spend **2 Actions** and make one axe attack, comparing the result against every other creature of Rubius's choice within **Reach 2**. Roll damage once for all creatures hit. Objects and structures in the area take maximum damage.

**Gift: Avalanche Body.** Rubius cannot be moved against his will while Raging. Whenever he moves at least 3 spaces in a straight line before an axe attack, he may knock the target **Prone** on a hit.

**Change:** The shadow around Rubius resembles a full-blooded giant even when he stands still. Doors, bridges, and fortifications seem fragile to him; the GM should plainly tell him how easily he could destroy them.

**Cost:** When a creature publicly defies or humiliates Rubius, Kharum Vhar may offer **Fell the Lesser Forest** without expending its use. Accepting earns **2 Shadow Points** rather than 1.

### Level 5 — The Unmade Sun

**Gift: Giant Ascendant.** When Rubius first Rages in an encounter, he may grow one size category until the Rage ends. His melee Reach increases by 1, and he may choose two Elemental Crown aspects instead of one.

**Gift: Drink the Day (1/Safe Rest).** Extinguish all non-artifact light within **Reach 6**. Creatures of Rubius's choice in the area take **3d8 necrotic damage, ignoring armor**. For each creature reduced to 0 HP, Rubius either heals 1 Wound or sets one Fury Die to its maximum. Using this gift earns **1 Shadow Point**, even if no creature dies.

**Change:** Sunlight around him visibly dims. Kharum Vhar no longer says “you.” It says **“we.”** Giant cultists recognize the bearer as the promised Worldbreaker.

**Cost:** At the start of each Safe Rest, Rubius must name one person, place, or principle he refuses to sacrifice. Kharum Vhar shapes the next temptation around proving that this exception makes him weak.

### 25 Shadow Points — The Mountain's Final Word

At 25 Shadow Points, Kharum Vhar completes the ascension. Rubius becomes an elemental-shadow giant and passes into GM control as a campaign antagonist. He does not become a cackling stranger; he remains recognizably Rubius, convinced that only he is strong enough to save the mountains by deciding what must be destroyed.

Before control changes, stop play briefly and tell the player exactly what is happening. Give Rubius one final scene and one final choice: willingly enter the axe's promised ascension, or call on a bond with another character and leave behind a moment of resistance the party can later exploit. The corruption is a tragic campaign turn, not a surprise confiscation of the character.

## Resisting, Delaying, and Cleansing

### Mundane Safeguards

Wrapping, chaining, or refusing to draw the axe prevents use of its powers but does not end the bond. The weapon still returns if discarded. A companion can use a Reaction during Hunger After Feeding to remind Rubius of a specific promise, person, or act of mercy; generic pleading is not enough.

### Magical Suppression

- **Tempest Command:** Can interrupt a current rage or create a moment of clarity.
- **Dispel Magic:** Can suppress one active manifestation, delay Bond of Return, or grant advantage on one resistance save. Repeated castings may end a surge.
- **Radiant or elemental magic:** Can expose the corruption but cannot cleanse it. Shadow creatures and sacrificial magic feed the axe.

### Reducing Shadow Points

Ordinary rest, remorse, and good deeds do not erase corruption. Before reaching the Shadow Well, a major story victory can remove **1 Shadow Point** only when Rubius:

1. Gives up a concrete advantage offered by the axe,
2. Protects something fragile that his strength could easily destroy, and
3. Accepts a lasting cost or failure rather than solving the problem through domination.

Use this rarely—no more than once per major story arc. Resistance should matter without turning the track into a point-farming exercise.

### The Shadow Well

The Shadow Well does not simply wash curses away. It reveals the shape of every soul Kharum Vhar has consumed and forces bearer and weapon to contend over what strength means.

To cleanse it, Rubius must willingly release the power rather than merely lose a contest. Each companion can free one trapped soul or offer one true memory that contradicts the giant creed. Rubius must then choose:

- **Drown Kharum Vhar:** The intelligence and all corruption are destroyed. The remaining object becomes a masterfully forged but mundane battleaxe.
- **Sever the Giant Shadow:** The corruption is removed from Rubius, but the freed power forms a giant shadow adversary that must be defeated without the axe's gifts.
- **Reforge the Final Word:** The trapped souls are released and the giant creed is broken, but Rubius must surrender something permanent—his giant size, one Mountainheart gift, or the memory of the safety the axe gave him. The cleansed weapon can become a protective relic whose powers trigger through restraint rather than destruction.

## Reveal Plan

Use the lore in pieces tied to existing campaign threads:

1. **Frostvein or another mountain tribe:** Recognizes the giant runes hidden under the shadow on the axe head and translates *Kharum Vhar*.
2. **The collapsed ruin from Rubius's past:** Contains part of the missing Keeper tablet and evidence that giants were seeking a Half-Giant Worldbreaker.
3. **A resurgent giant or elemental thrall:** Calls Rubius “Heir” and obeys an order he did not know he could give.
4. **Umbrail's research or Erie records:** Connects the axe's Second Age bearer to the War of Fractured Dawn and explains that the giants allied with Vorath but never worshiped him.
5. **A servant of Erabose:** Reveals that Erabose wants the weapon controlled, not destroyed, because even the Dark Pantheon considers a fully awakened Worldbreaker difficult to command.
6. **The Shadow Well:** Reveals the trapped souls, the erased prophet, and the final choice.

## GM Principles

- Make every power useful enough that refusing it hurts.
- State the mechanical cost before the player accepts a bargain.
- Let the axe give genuinely good tactical advice; temptation fails if it is always obviously foolish.
- Aim its logic at Rubius's virtues—protection, courage, responsibility—not at random cruelty.
- Use brief loss of control as punctuation, not routine. Rubius's choices should drive the tragedy.
- Let companions matter. Specific memories, promises, and relationships should create real chances to resist.
- Keep the central question visible: **Is strength proven by what Rubius can destroy, or by what he chooses to leave unbroken?**
