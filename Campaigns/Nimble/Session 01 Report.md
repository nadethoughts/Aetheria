# Nimble Session 01 Report

## Executive Summary

Session 01 introduced the Nimble rules, the world of Aetheria, the town of Clackston, and the five original members of the adventuring party. A dangerous pattern of livestock and grain thefts threatened Clackston's ability to survive the coming winter, prompting Master Glenson to organize a paid posse. Rubius Haggard, Whisper, Umbrail, Rowan Hearthboro, and Alder each answered the call for different personal reasons.

The investigation quickly produced evidence that the thieves were black-scaled kobolds. Southeast of town, the party intercepted a lone kobold dragging a stolen mule. Rather than killing it, the group restrained and questioned it. The captive revealed that the kobolds were operating from a large ditch or crater to the south, where there was a door and a powerful "big boss." Rowan released the kobold with a warning after the party recovered the mule.

Following the trail led the party to what appeared to be an old impact crater. The ground contained black, glasslike material, and an ancient stone doorway stood within the site. An ominous inscription warned the "Keepers of the Dawn" against a thing inside that "drinks the light." The party ended the session at the threshold of a hidden Second Age complex that had become a kobold warren.

The session established several threads that later became central to the campaign: the connection between the kobolds and shadow, the buried ruins of the Second Age, Rubius's search for evidence of a giant enclave, Umbrail's knowledge of old languages, and the party's preference for questioning potentially useful enemies before resorting to execution.

## Detailed Chronological Report

### 1. Campaign and System Introduction

The table began with an overview of Nimble as a game built on a recognizable fantasy d20 foundation while changing combat, character options, and action economy. The campaign was placed in **Aetheria**, with the opening adventure centered on **Clackston**, a farming community in the Verdant Strand.

The GM introduced the broad class options available in the system. The discussion was primarily instructional, but it also established the mechanical identities that would shape the party: direct physical power, protective intervention, flexible spellcasting, shadow magic, speed, and opportunistic attacks.

### 2. The Original Party Was Created

Five adventurers answered Clackston's call:

- **Rubius Haggard**, a Half-Giant Berserker, is approximately thirteen feet tall, red-haired, green-eyed, and unusually friendly for someone of his size and strength. He wants to prove that he is not defined by the reputation of other giants and is searching for evidence of a lost giant enclave.
- **Whisper**, a human Cheat, is motivated by excitement and a desire to escape boredom. His later history suggests that leaving home also involved a stolen family-related object, but that detail was not yet fully developed here.
- **Umbrail**, an elderly Birdfolk Mage, left a dwindling mountain people to see the wider world before age closed that opportunity. His exact "bad choice" background detail remained undefined.
- **Rowan Hearthboro**, a Bun Bun Oathsworn, had spent much of his life helping a farming community. He trained a successor before leaving to help people beyond his home.
- **Alder**, a Shroomling Shadowmancer and homesteader, had a direct stake in the thefts because his own livestock was among the animals taken.

The party's first interaction occurred around the posse gathering. The scene allowed the characters to introduce themselves while establishing the contrast between their appearances and temperaments: a towering but genial giant, a guarded rogue, an old flying mage, a duty-driven rabbitfolk protector, and a mushroom person commanding unsettling shadow magic.

### 3. Master Glenson Explained the Crisis

**Master Glenson** organized the posse. He was described as a gruff man in his mid-fifties with salt-and-pepper coloring, a crutch, and an injured or stiff leg. His condition prevented him from leading the search personally, a fact he clearly disliked.

Glenson reported that Clackston's four largest farms had suffered repeated thefts of animals and feed:

- Glenson's own farm
- George Ballack's farm
- Fred Fendroy's farm
- Patricia Keel's farm

The losses were not yet catastrophic, but continued thefts would leave the town without enough food or livestock to endure winter. Small black scales and clawed tracks had been recovered near the affected properties.

Glenson offered **25 gold pieces per person**, with a **5-gold bonus** if the group resolved the problem within one week. The party accepted the job and began its investigation southeast of town.

### 4. The Party Followed the Kobold Trail

The party examined the fields and recovered enough evidence to identify the culprits as kobolds. Rowan's Nature Craft knowledge was particularly useful in interpreting the black scales and clawed tracks.

Umbrail later spotted a black-scaled kobold trying to force a stolen mule southward. The mule was resisting strongly, slowing the thief and giving the party time to act.

Rubius attempted to tackle the kobold. The maneuver was more enthusiastic than precise, but the group successfully captured the creature without killing it. Rowan applied manacles and led the questioning, while the party calmed and recovered the mule.

### 5. The Captured Kobold Revealed the Warren

Under questioning, the kobold admitted that its people were taking livestock and supplies. It described a large ditch or crater farther south, a doorway within it, and a powerful "big boss" directing the warren.

This became the session's first important moral and tactical choice. The group could have killed the kobold, taken it back to town, or used it as a guide. Instead, Rowan released it after warning it not to continue preying on Clackston. The decision avoided unnecessary bloodshed but also allowed the warren to receive possible warning that armed investigators were approaching.

### 6. The Crater and Ancient Doorway

The trail led to a large depression that appeared to have been formed by a meteor or some other violent impact. Black, obsidianlike glass covered portions of the site. The crater exposed a stone doorway belonging to a much older structure, apparently untouched until the impact revealed it.

The party recognized that the kobold thefts were no longer merely a local animal-rustling problem. The creatures had occupied an ancient ruin and were operating under a larger leader. The location also offered a possible connection to Rubius's interest in old ruins and lost giant history, though no evidence of giants was found in this session.

The entrance bore a warning:

> Turn Back, Keepers of the Dawn  
> Within Lies the Shadow of the Sun  
> Touch Not the Thing that Drinks the Light  
> Lest Your Soul Be Sung into Night

The warning connected the ruin to ancient conflict and dangerous shadow power. The session ended with the party at the door, prepared to enter the kobold warren in Session 02.

## Decisions Made During the Session

### Firm or Nearly Firm Decisions

- All five original characters joined Glenson's posse.
- The party accepted the offered payment and one-week bonus terms.
- The group followed the kobold trail south instead of remaining near the farms.
- The captured kobold was questioned rather than immediately killed.
- Rowan released the kobold after recovering the mule and learning the warren's location.
- The party continued to the crater despite the warning on the doorway.

### Important Decision Points Considered

- **How to respond to the thefts:** The party treated the disappearances as an organized threat rather than ordinary animal loss.
- **Capture or kill the kobold:** Capturing it yielded the location of the warren and preserved the possibility of mercy.
- **What to do with the prisoner:** Release created a moral precedent but introduced the risk of warning the enemy.
- **Enter or heed the warning:** The session ended before the physical entry, but the party's intent was clearly to continue.

## Character-by-Character Impact

### Rubius Haggard

Rubius established himself as physically imposing but socially warm. His attempt to tackle the kobold showed his instinct for direct action, while the ancient ruin offered the first possible lead in his search for forgotten giant history. He also began the campaign with a strong need to demonstrate that being a giant does not make him cruel.

### Whisper

Whisper joined primarily in search of excitement. The job immediately delivered danger and mystery, but his personal history remained mostly concealed. His practical, opportunistic skill set positioned him to become especially important once the party entered the ruin.

### Umbrail

Umbrail's age, flight, magical training, and connection to older cultures distinguished him from the rest of the group. His aerial observation helped locate the kobold, while the doorway's ancient language and warning created a natural role for his knowledge in the next session.

### Rowan Hearthboro

Rowan became the party's moral and protective center. He interpreted the tracks, restrained and questioned the kobold, and chose mercy once the immediate danger was contained. That decision defined his Oathsworn identity before the party had fought a full battle.

### Alder

Alder had the most direct personal reason to accept the job because his own livestock had been taken. The discovery that the thieves occupied a shadow-linked ruin also placed his magic in potentially dangerous thematic proximity to the threat.

## Overall Assessment

Session 01 successfully moved from a grounded village problem into a larger supernatural mystery. The missing livestock gave every character a clear reason to cooperate, while the crater and inscription promised that the apparent kobold raid was only the surface of an older danger. The choice to spare the captured kobold also established that this party would not automatically treat every enemy as disposable.

The strongest campaign seeds were the contrast between light and shadow, the possibility that ancient history had physically resurfaced, and the mismatch between ordinary kobolds and the ominous power implied by the doorway. The next session needed to test the newly formed group's teamwork and determine whether the warning described the kobolds' weapon, their leader, or something far more dangerous within the complex.
