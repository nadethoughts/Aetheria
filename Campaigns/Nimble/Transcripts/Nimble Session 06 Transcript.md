## 1. The Griffin Fight

The session resumed at the ruined Erie outpost, where a griffin was attacking the party near the edge of the mountain. The creature was still airborne, leaving most of the party without an easy way to reach it.

### Assessing the Griffin

**Player:** Are griffins normally found here in the cold mountains? What is the origin of this griffin? Why is it here?

**GM:** Griffins are not typically mountain creatures. They are more common in hills and open fields. It is not unheard of to find one in the mountains, but it is not common.

The group reviewed the **Help**, **Assess**, and **Create an Opening** actions. Help is a reaction, while Assess can be used to obtain useful information or grant an advantage when the character can justify how that knowledge helps.

**Player:** I want to use lore to remember a weakness. The wing joint is vulnerable. If we damage the wing, it will not be able to move or fly as well. I want to create an opening for whoever attacks next.

**GM:** That works. The next person able to capitalize on that weakness can gain the advantage.

The party still had to solve the immediate problem: the griffin was in the air, and most of the characters could not reach it.

### Rowan Draws Its Attention

Rowan moved into a more exposed position and attempted to draw the griffin's attention away from his allies.

**Rowan:** I am bigger than most of the people here—other than the giant—and I am going to persuade it to come at me. I want its attention on me instead of everybody else.

**GM:** You can use Insight to create that opening. You succeed. The griffin's eyes are trained on Rowan, so an ally gains advantage.

Rowan's fearlessness also meant he was not intimidated by the charging creature.

### Umbrail Supports the Party

Umbrail used **True Strike** to prepare Lil Chang and Alder for their next attacks. The table clarified that multiple uses of True Strike cannot be stacked on the same character because of an erratum.

Umbrail then moved close enough to use the party's recently acquired **Flamecaller's Talisman**.

**Umbrail:** I use the talisman to conjure a gust of flame and light one of the griffin's feathers. It is now Smoldering.

The talisman required a depletion roll when used. The roll came up as a 1.

**GM:** The talisman is destroyed.

**Umbrail:** It had a good run. A short one, but a good run.

Umbrail immediately followed with **Ignite**, a level-one spell that deals additional damage to a Smoldering target and then ends the condition. With two sources of advantage, he rolled 22 damage.

**GM:** The griffin bursts into flame in midair and screeches in pain.

### Rubius Throws the Shadow Axe

Rubius wanted to strike the damaged wing, but his battle axe did not have a listed throwing range. The table made a temporary ruling that an improvised thrown weapon could travel a number of squares equal to the thrower's Strength.

Rubius spent two actions moving close enough and then hurled his axe.

**Rubius:** I am aiming at the burned wing. I want to make it come down.

The attack dealt 15 damage. The axe grazed the griffin and went over the edge of the mountain.

**GM:** It could have been worse. You could have missed entirely and still thrown the axe over the edge.

The table clarified that Rubius had thrown the shadow axe rather than his ordinary battle axe. The shadow axe was back in his possession later in the session, although the recording never establishes whether it was recovered normally or returned through its own magic.

### The Griffin Dives on Lil Chang

The wounded griffin descended and attacked Lil Chang with one of its claws.

**GM:** It hits for 18 damage, and the target is grappled.

Rowan immediately used **Interpose** and **Defend**, taking the attack in Lil Chang's place. His defense reduced the damage substantially, apparently to 4, although the overlapping table talk makes the final damage assignment difficult to hear. Rowan ended up grappled by the griffin.

### Alder Forces It Back

Alder commanded both of his shadow minions to attack. Together they dealt 13 damage. He followed with **Shadow Blast** for another 12 damage.

**GM:** The griffin cries out in pain, releases Rowan, and begins backing away. It is making distressed calling noises and pawing at the ground. It no longer appears eager to continue the fight.

The party paused rather than finishing it off.

## 2. More Griffins on the Horizon

As the wounded griffin retreated, the party finally noticed several more shapes approaching through the sky. Approximately six griffins were closing on the ruined outpost, each carrying an orc rider.

### Immediate Reactions

**GM:** You can each have a reaction before they arrive.

**Lil Chang:** I look at the wounded griffin and demand, “Why are you here?”

The griffin showed a flicker of comprehension but could not answer Lil Chang in a way he understood. It looked between Umbrail and Lil Chang and made an unclear gesture.

**Rowan:** I fall into a defensive stance. Then I Assess the riders. Do any of these orcs carry the broken-fang symbol we found on the hostile orc bodies?

**GM:** No. You do not see that symbol on any of them.

Umbrail tried to read the riders' formation and prepare the group against a possible attack, but his Assess roll failed.

Rubius was still enraged, which imposed a penalty on the careful use of Nature Craft. Despite the penalty, he rolled well enough to interpret the wounded griffin's posture.

**GM:** The griffin considers Umbrail an ally and Lil Chang a threat.

Understanding that the situation might not be another fight caused Rubius's rage to subside. His shadowy aura dissipated.

Alder, meanwhile, remained low in the pit among the bodies and tried to make himself less conspicuous.

### Umbrail Speaks with the Griffin

Once the party was no longer acting in strict combat order, Umbrail approached the wounded creature. It avoided the characters it distrusted but allowed the birdfolk mage to come closer.

Umbrail's knowledge of griffin speech was rusty, and the conversation sounded like squawking and bird chatter to the rest of the group.

**Umbrail:** What is happening here? I have been away for several years.

**Griffin:** Too much to tell. Wait for my friends. They will explain.

The griffin indicated the approaching riders.

An offer to treat its wounds was rejected. The griffin preferred to remain injured rather than trust one of the people who had just attacked it, describing the would-be healer as a “bad man.”

**Party:** Apparently the incoming riders are its friends. We should wait and see what they have to say.

## 3. Zing Zow and the Cloud Seers

The griffin riders descended onto the outpost.

The orcs were dressed differently from those the party had encountered earlier. Instead of hides, they wore woven tunics in pale blue and royal blue. Their war paint used the same colors in swirling patterns.

The lead rider dismounted. He kept one hand ready near his battle axe, scanned the battlefield, and looked each party member in the eye. His gaze lingered on Umbrail before moving to Lil Chang.

**Orc Rider:** Lil Chang? It has been years. I hardly recognize you.

The rider was an old childhood friend from Lil Chang's time in the mountains. At the table, he was named **Zing Zow** of the **Cloud Seer tribe**.

**Zing Zow:** What brings you back to our mountain after all these years?

**Lil Chang:** We came into the mountains because of the kobolds. Then we found this battlefield and the destroyed outpost. We are trying to determine what happened here. Do you know anything about it?

**Zing Zow:** I do not know the precise details. We were coming to help, but it appears that we arrived too late. We knew the outpost was under attack. A call to arms went out to our tribe.

The Cloud Seers and the Erie had not always been close allies, but they now faced a common enemy.

**Zing Zow:** The enemy of my enemy is my friend. There are powers at work in these mountains. Things that were away are no longer away.

## 4. Razor's War in the Mountains

### The Dragon

**Zing Zow:** A wyrm has come to our mountains.

The table clarified that “wyrm” meant a dragon.

**Zing Zow:** It came from the hill country to the southwest. It appears to be amassing an army. Those of us who do not wish to serve must resist.

The dragon's name was **Razor**, explicitly spelled R-A-Z-O-R. Razor had established a roost in the southern mountains.

Zing Zow explained that Umbrail's people guarded objects that would be extremely dangerous in a dragon's possession. Protecting the Erie was therefore not simply an act of friendship; it was necessary to prevent Razor from gaining enough power to dominate the entire mountain range.

Umbrail remembered the history between the Erie and the dragon flights. His people had fought the dragons in the past and protected both ancient secrets and objects of power to prevent dragons from settling permanently in the region.

### Razor's Coalition

The kobolds the party originally came to investigate were no longer the central problem.

**Zing Zow:** Nearly every kobold in the mountains has turned its attention to Razor. They treat him almost as a god. The kobolds you encountered were probably deserters.

The speed with which the kobolds joined Razor was unsettling. It was almost as though they had been waiting for a dragon to arrive.

One orc clan had also joined Razor: the **Black Fang orcs**. Zing Zow believed their arrangement with the dragon was an alliance of convenience rather than worship.

Other orc tribes remained uncommitted. They were waiting to see which side would prevail before choosing an allegiance.

The present alignments were therefore:

- The Erie and the Cloud Seers were resisting Razor.
- The Black Fang orcs and nearly all mountain kobolds served Razor.
- Several other orc tribes remained neutral.

**Zing Zow:** An ally today does not mean an ally tomorrow when dealing with a dragon. Once a dragon gains a foothold, its greed takes over. If Razor's conquest succeeds here, I do not believe it will stop at the mountains.

### Razor Is Not a Normal Combat Objective

The party discussed whether they could become strong enough to confront Razor directly.

**GM:** To be clear, this is not a matter of gaining a few levels and then fighting the dragon. Razor is closer to a level-20 monstrosity. You will not be able to handle him anytime soon, and even at level 20 success would not be guaranteed. Think of this more like the situation with Smaug in *The Hobbit*.

Zing Zow reinforced the warning. Multiple companies of warriors had already tried to face Razor, including alliances of birdfolk and orcs. None had returned.

The party accepted that walking into Razor's roost would accomplish little beyond giving the dragon a quick meal—and potentially delivering one of the artifacts he wanted.

## 5. The Mine and the Erie Artifacts

### The Nearby Mine

Zing Zow had heard that a mine near the ruined outpost was especially important to the Erie. It was approximately a thirty-minute walk away.

Umbrail did not know every secret surrounding the mine, but he remembered the broad reason it mattered: the mine contained an unusual ore said to empower draconic breath weapons.

**Umbrail:** It is critical that Razor not gain access to this mine. If he does, this war may be lost before it goes any further.

**Zing Zow:** My riders and I can secure the mine, assuming it has not already been taken. We can also send word to our clan leader and request a larger force.

This allowed the party to leave the immediate military problem to trained local allies while considering what else they could do.

### The Distributed Artifacts

The Erie did not keep all of their objects of power in one place. Umbrail knew of several significant sites:

1. The greatest of the artifacts was kept in the Erie capital.
2. Another was hidden in an underground vault beneath the ancient battlefield where the decisive blow of the Dragon War had been struck.
3. A further artifact was kept on a peak in the eastern Balshar Mountains, associated with the resting place of the Erie general who defeated the dragons.
4. Lesser objects of power had been distributed among important members of the Erie.

### Umbrail's Mentor and the Unknown Artifact

The conversation led to a new realization about the possessions Umbrail had taken when he left home.

**Umbrail's Player:** Last session, Umbrail found his mentor's corpse on the battlefield. I think the mentor was the bearer of one of the lesser objects of power. There are the great artifacts, but there are also lesser ones distributed among important Erie. They are not advertised as objects of power. When Umbrail left, he stole or took some things in his haste, and one of those items was the artifact. He did not understand what it was.

**GM:** That works perfectly.

Umbrail now understood that he might be carrying something Razor wanted, but he did not know exactly what the item did or to whom it should be returned.

**Table:** Preferably not Razor—or anyone else of dragonkind.

### General Brand

Zing Zow explained that the Erie war effort was being directed by **General Brand** from the Erie city, the people's greatest fortress.

Brand did not remain in the capital continuously. He frequently traveled into the eastern Balshar Mountains for weeks at a time and appeared to follow a repeated circuit through the region. Zing Zow did not know the purpose of those journeys.

The capital therefore offered two reasons for the party to visit: it was the center of the war effort, and General Brand might know what to do with Umbrail's artifact.

## 6. Choosing the Party's Next Objective

The party identified three immediate goals:

1. Convince the unaligned orc tribes to join the resistance.
2. Keep Razor away from the mine.
3. Find an Erie authority who could take responsibility for Umbrail's artifact.

Zing Zow and the Cloud Seer riders had already volunteered to address the mine. The party therefore debated whether to go directly to the Erie capital or attempt to build an alliance first.

### Why the Orc Tribes Had Not United

**Zing Zow:** We have tried to convince the other tribes to join us, but they are resistant. They are waiting to see where the pieces fall before committing themselves.

Generations of old feuds made cooperation among the orc tribes difficult. The Cloud Seers had been able to ally with the Erie because there had been enough distance between them to avoid serious conflict, but relations with neighboring orcs were more complicated.

**Zing Zow:** We are not always good neighbors. Old grudges get in the way of new alliances. Perhaps you will succeed where we could not.

The party recognized that its status as an outside group could be an advantage. They were not responsible for those historical grievances and might be able to speak to each tribe without reopening an old feud.

### The Case for Returning with Allies

Umbrail initially favored reaching someone in authority, but another possibility emerged: returning to his people with an army or alliance at his back might make his difficult homecoming easier.

**Party:** If nobody else has managed to unite the tribes because of old grudges, an outside perspective may be exactly what is needed. We are nothing if not outsiders.

The table also observed that arriving at the capital with additional allies would demonstrate the party's value and might make the Erie more forgiving of Umbrail's past.

Not everyone was immediately convinced. Alder, in particular, approached the problem pragmatically.

**Alder:** Why are we doing this? Why not go somewhere dark, underground, and safe?

The group acknowledged that it could not defeat Razor itself. The answer was to contribute indirectly: strengthen the resistance, deny Razor easy allies, and avoid delivering Umbrail's artifact into the dragon's claws.

### Final Direction

At the end-of-session check-in, the GM asked the group to state its intended direction.

**GM:** It sounds like you plan to focus on getting the other orc tribes allied with the Erie. Is that correct?

The group agreed, with a few reluctant or skeptical voices. They had chosen a strategy, but not yet a specific destination. No first tribe, travel route, or diplomatic offer was selected during this session.

## 7. Level Three

Before ending the session, the party advanced to level 3.

### General Advancement

The table reviewed the level-up procedure:

- Each character's hit die increased by one step as appropriate for the level.
- Each player rolled hit dice with advantage and added the higher result to maximum HP.
- Each character gained one skill point.
- A character could move one existing skill point to another skill.
- Level 3 opened subclass or path features.

Some of the following choices were still being explored aloud, so the character sheets remain the final authority.

### Alder — Eldritch Mushroom

**Alder's Player:** Eldritch Mushroom.

Alder's new features included the ability to maintain three shadow minions and make one of them more independent, allowing it to remain outside combat and perform simple tasks. The table leaned into the feature's explicitly “very creepy” description.

### Umbrail — Invoker of Chaos

Umbrail discussed **Invoker of Chaos**. The path allows him to spend one less mana when casting a spell. If he chooses to do so and the spell scores a critical result, the GM rolls on the hidden Chaos Table.

**Umbrail's Player:** Unless I specify otherwise, assume I am casting with one less mana.

**GM:** Then we need to remember to invoke chaos whenever you crit.

The GM explained only that a result of 1 on the Chaos Table is very bad and a result of 20 is excellent. The rest remains secret.

Umbrail also discussed newly gained access to cold spells. Since he could already cast level-one spells, the table ruled that gaining knowledge of cold magic gave him access to level-one cold spells.

### Lil Chang — Way of Pain

Lil Chang reviewed **Way of Pain**.

Once per round, he can turn a melee attack against him into a critical hit. When critically hit, he reduces the incoming damage by half and deals the amount he actually suffered back to the attacker, ignoring armor. He can also suffer a wound to double the reflected damage.

The table regarded the ability as extremely powerful but dangerous, especially because intentionally accepting wounds has serious long-term costs.

### Rubius — Mountain Heart

Rubius reviewed **Mountain Heart**, the option that best matched his half-giant identity.

The path improves his resilience when spending Fury Dice to reduce incoming damage. It also allows him to convert sufficient healing from expended Hit Dice into wound recovery, giving him a limited way to heal wounds outside a safe rest.

The alternative path, Red Mist, had been discussed previously, but Mountain Heart remained the thematic favorite.

### Rowan — Refuge

Rowan considered Vengeance and **Refuge**, with Refuge fitting the group's “full tank” direction.

Refuge improves Rowan's shield and expands the range at which he can use Interpose. The table clarified that the larger aura does not grant unlimited Interpose reactions; it only expands the area in which the normal Interpose action can protect an ally.

### Whisper

No clear subclass selection for Whisper was captured in the recording.

## 8. Closing Discussion

### The Shadow Axe

After the party settled on alliance-building as its next objective, the table returned briefly to Rubius's shadow axe.

Rubius's player expressed an eventual desire to get rid of it, especially once its negative effects became visible. The GM emphasized the distinction between player knowledge and character knowledge.

**GM:** From Rubius's perspective, the axe has been wonderful. It deals extra damage. It returned to him after being thrown. It has been friendly. Your character does not yet have a reason to believe it is dangerous.

The party joked that an intelligent weapon speaking to its wielder might simply be friendly—especially in a group where Alder already surrounds himself with far creepier shadows.

The GM confirmed that removing the axe is possible, but that there is a path the characters will have to discover before they can do so.

### Campaign Direction

The table briefly discussed the expected length and rhythm of the Nimble game. The group had originally discussed a shorter run of roughly six sessions, but the players were enjoying the system and the campaign had already continued beyond that point. No firm end date was established.

The game concluded after the level-up and planning discussion.
