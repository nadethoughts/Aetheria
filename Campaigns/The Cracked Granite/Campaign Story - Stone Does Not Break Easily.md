# Stone Does Not Break Easily

*A novella of The Cracked Granite*

## Chapter One — The King's Quiet Summons

The Fourth Age was young, but the Stonefang Mountains had already learned not to trust the sky.

When the [[World/Events/Flaming Retribution|Flaming Retribution]] burned kingdoms from the world, [[World/Locations/Ironhold/Ironhold|Ironhold]] closed its gates. Granite, law, and memory protected the dwarves while nations beyond the mountain fractured. A year passed before the great doors opened again.

The first news from the wider world came as silence.

[[World/Locations/Ironhold/Dagger's Sheath|Dagger's Sheath]], Ironhold's southern colony, stopped sending messages.

King Hargrin Deepmantle could not march an army without frightening the clans, alerting Ironhold's enemies, and giving political rivals a reason to claim that the throne had lost control. So he sent for three dwarves whose reputations allowed them to move where soldiers could not.

Hurgan Ironmark had been born to the Night Watch. He understood a mountain pass by the way wind moved through it and could find danger in darkness by the quality of the silence. Demlor Ironmark was a hunter and investigator whose body answered the metal sleeping in stone. Iron came to his hands without forge or hammer. Rurik Embercloak carried a firearm, a bag of patient tricks, and the brittle pride of a clan declared unworthy before he had been given any say in the matter.

They met the king in a rune-carved chamber few citizens of Ironhold knew existed. With him sat the great voices of the realm: Baelgrin Deepdelve, keeper of history and magic; Bramli Stoneguard, captain and loyal shield; Maela Blackshard, mistress of secrets; and Varric Silvervein, ambitious, sharp-tongued, and always ready to explain why danger was someone else's exaggeration.

The king gave them sealed authority and one command: discover what happened to Dagger's Sheath.

Bramli stopped them afterward.

“The council is compromised,” he warned. “Do not assume the danger lies only beyond our walls.”

They left at midnight on mountain goats. Before departing, Rurik ate one last meal at the Forge's Fire and left a dwarf carved from soap upon the table. It was a small, foolish promise that he intended to return.

Days later, Dagger's Sheath appeared below the final ridge.

The colony had been destroyed.

Buildings lay crushed. Stone had been scorched. The royal seal was broken. Dwarves remained inside split armor, some flattened beneath blows no ogre could have delivered. Footprints crossed the quarry, each large enough to hold a body.

Demlor studied them until denial became impossible.

Giants.

The old enemy had been extinct for a thousand years.

Something skittered in the mine.

## Chapter Two — Fire in the Quarry

Cinder rats came first, creatures of molten stone that carried fire in their wounds. Rurik's cold-forged ammunition broke their burning bodies. Demlor pulled blades from the mountain itself. Hurgan's weapons rose and fell with the rhythm of long practice.

After the last rat died, voices drifted from the mine.

Two ogres complained that the dwarven tunnels were too small. Between them walked an Azer shaped like a dwarf and made of living fire. They were searching for a missing captive on behalf of masters they feared.

The three dwarves struck before they were seen.

They killed the ogres and broke the elemental smith, then followed the ruin deeper. There they found evidence of a desperate last stand and, beneath collapsed stone, Captain Kael Ironmark.

Kael lived because Ironhold's soldiers were difficult to kill and because the mountain had not fallen quite far enough.

They carried him out while the mine collapsed behind them.

His report was worse than the ruins. Giants had returned with ogres, elemental servants, and magic that could transform captured dwarves into obedient thralls. Survivors from Dagger's Sheath were being held in a war camp west of the colony.

From a ridge above that camp, the party counted cages, siege weapons, patrols, and ritual fires. Twenty dwarves waited behind bars. Ogres guarded them. Azers tended the forges. Mud elementals moved between tents. A commander named Gorvok the Maul ruled the encampment even when he was not physically present.

An open attack would have been suicide.

The dwarves chose theft, sabotage, and fire.

## Chapter Three — The Ember Guard

Night reduced the ogres' world to torchlight.

Hurgan traced a route along the cliff where loose stone could hide careful feet. Rurik slipped into the command tent and stole maps and correspondence from the war table. He left a marble behind, because a successful theft deserved a signature.

Then he set the black powder.

The explosion lifted canvas into the sky. Ogres ran toward fire. Orders crossed in the dark. Patrols abandoned their stations.

Demlor reached into the command tent with magnetic force and pulled a mound of stolen dwarven weapons toward him. A female ogre ritualist saw the metal move and attacked. Behind her, a captive dwarf hung in chains halfway through an elemental transformation.

Demlor fought amid smoke, steel, and broken ritual light. He killed the ritualist and shattered the captive's chains before the magic could erase what remained of the dwarf's mind.

At the cages, Hurgan cut down the guards. The three heroes armed the prisoners with their own recovered weapons and led twenty-one survivors up the cliff before the camp understood where they had gone.

Captain Kael waited at the hidden rendezvous.

The intelligence stolen by Rurik confirmed that the attacks were larger than Dagger's Sheath. The giants were organizing. The elemental thralls were not accidents. Someone had designed a method of turning captives into soldiers who obeyed without fear, loyalty, or memory.

The party began the forced march toward the nearest watchtower.

Behind them, Gorvok released his hunters.

## Chapter Four — Two Days to Hammergate

Twenty-one refugees could not move as quickly as three heroes.

The pursuit found them in the high valleys. Dire wolves came first, led by an ogre twisted into a werewolf. The refugees huddled against stone while Hurgan, Demlor, and Rurik held the trail. One dwarf fell beneath claw and tooth. Rurik dragged him back from death while the others killed the beast.

They crossed beneath the hunting circle of a flame drake and passed close enough to a cyclops and its giant spider companion to hear them moving through the trees. For most of the journey, Hurgan's choices kept the refugees alive.

Near the end, exhaustion made one wrong step sufficient.

The cyclops dropped from the trunk of a fallen tree. Its spider spat web across the trail. Rurik fired into the giant's eye. Hurgan opened the artery in its leg. Demlor filled the spider with metal, and Rurik finished it with a blast that took its remaining eyes and what little mind lay behind them.

[[World/Locations/Ironhold/Hammergate Hold|Hammergate Hold]] opened its doors to the survivors.

Captain Thrain Emberhelm listened to the report and put the tower on alert. Messages went out to the other sentinels. For the first time in a millennium, dwarven warnings carried the word *giant* as fact rather than legend.

One tower did not answer.

[[World/Locations/Ironhold/Sentinel’s Rise|Sentinel's Rise]] had gone silent.

The party went to investigate.

They found it razed. Troll tracks crossed the ruins beside the marks of a heavy, furred creature no one could name. Inside lay an order bearing Grand Regent Varric Silvervein's authority: lock the tower down, ignore messages, and do not reinforce neighboring holds.

The order had turned a watchtower into a tomb.

The party warned Hammergate through a sending stone. Then, suspecting that enemy ears might be listening, they sent false information about their own position and began moving toward Ironhold by another route.

At twilight they saw the [[World/Items/Artifacts/The Rings of Coram|Rings of Coram]] shining.

## Chapter Five — The Rings Awaken

The Rings had stood since before dwarven memory, four gates of carved stone arranged around a sacred center. Scholars called them a monument because no one knew what else to call a machine that had forgotten its purpose.

Now each ring burned with a different color.

A cave giant stood at the center, speaking words the dwarves could feel through the ground. Four weakened trolls waited at the cardinal gates. One by one, they stepped through stone and emerged remade: forest, frost, cavern, rock—flesh rewritten by elemental power.

The giant was not summoning an army.

It was manufacturing one.

The dwarves attacked.

Rurik fired from concealment until the ancient site echoed like a foundry under siege. Hurgan met the cave giant at close range, smoking axe and warhammer striking wherever ritual armor failed. Demlor opened the metal gate within himself and filled the air with shards.

They killed one troll before its transformation and fought the others after the Rings made them stronger. The forest troll burned. The frost troll left ice in every wound. The cavern troll hurled stone from beyond reach. The giant abandoned its ritual and entered the fight with the confidence of a creature that believed history guaranteed its victory.

History was wrong.

When the last enemy fell, Demlor used a recording rod to capture the bodies, the transformed trolls, the awakened Rings, and the unmistakable shape of a dead giant. They searched the giant's possessions and added maps and correspondence to the evidence already taken from the ogre camp and Sentinel's Rise.

Someone in Ironhold had been directing attention south while a far greater force gathered elsewhere.

Someone had ordered the watchtowers blind.

They returned to the mountain city carrying proof.

## Chapter Six — The Traitor in the Hall

Ironhold was preparing for the wrong war.

Soldiers mustered toward the southern passes. Isolationists filled public chambers and demanded that the gates be closed again. The three heroes caused a minor riot merely by walking through the city alive; official reports had placed them trapped far away, waiting for rescue.

They asked for the king.

Weapons were surrendered at the keep. Royal guards escorted them into the council chamber.

Varric Silvervein sat beside the throne.

King Hargrin looked relieved, then confused. “We were told you were trapped at Sentinel's Rise. How did you escape?”

Demlor looked around the room.

“A traitor sits within it.”

Varric's eyes narrowed.

The party presented the recording. The king watched trolls step through the Rings and emerge transformed. He saw the cave giant. Fear entered his face—not cowardice, but the clear understanding that a childhood nightmare had become military fact.

Varric dismissed it as illusion.

Rurik produced the stolen battle map. Demlor produced the lockdown order. Hurgan spoke for the refugees and the dead. Varric twisted every piece of evidence toward the south. Ogres were real, he insisted. Giants were extinct. The party was unreliable. Ironhold must commit its full army where he had always advised.

The king summoned Baelgrin Deepdelve.

The archmage examined the recording rod with the annoyance of a master craftsman asked to identify a nail. Then he confirmed that its image could not have been altered.

The giant was real.

The documents were real.

Varric's certainty began to look like knowledge.

The king ordered scouts north and northeast while reinforcing the south without stripping Ironhold's defenses. He ordered Varric's properties and correspondence searched.

The truth emerged quickly.

For at least a year, [[World/Characters/Ironhold Territory/Varric Silvervein|Varric]] had been writing to **[[World/Characters/Vargram/Grondar Earthcaller|Grondar Earthcaller]]**. He had promised to deliver Ironhold as a conquered kingdom. In exchange, Grondar would place Varric over the dwarves as a puppet king.

Varric had not underestimated the threat.

He had prepared the kingdom to lose.

## Chapter Seven — The Granite Crown

The northern scouts found an army larger than the southern force.

Beyond it, where the Flaming Retribution had shifted coastlines and raised new land, stood an island that had not existed before. **[[World/Locations/Far Grand|Far Grand]]** had risen from the changed sea, and giants occupied it. Their forces were already moving toward the mountains when Ironhold discovered them.

Secrecy ended.

The giants began open war.

They had allied with princes of the elemental planes. Their rituals empowered trolls, shaped ogres, and emptied captured dwarves into living tools. The Azer at Dagger's Sheath had been a true elemental. The dwarven thralls were something worse: people still alive after everything that made them people had been taken away.

The three heroes had not prevented the war.

They had prevented Ironhold from losing it before knowing it had begun.

King Hargrin named them **Wardens of the Granite Crown**. Among ordinary dwarves, another name had already taken hold: the **Ember Guard of Dagger's Sheath**.

Rurik asked that his clan be restored to the Geargrinds and that the accusation against his father be investigated honestly. He joined the war not merely as a sniper but as an inventor, raising new weapons from old disgrace.

Demlor studied the rescued dwarf whose transformation had been interrupted. With Baelgrin, he began searching for a way to grant willing dwarves elemental strength without erasing their minds. When the kingdom required delicate violence, stolen plans, or ancient knowledge recovered from impossible places, he went back into the field.

Hurgan looked north toward Far Grand. City walls had never been his calling. He crossed into giant territory, learned the paths of a land the world had only just created, and took battle plans from tables where giants believed no dwarf could stand.

They fought together when they could. Apart when they had to.

The war continued beyond the tale told here. Ironhold would win or fall by more hands than three. But its people entered that war with open eyes, a living king, an unbroken army, and heroes who had already walked into the enemy's camp and returned with its secrets.

Granite had cracked.

It had not broken.
